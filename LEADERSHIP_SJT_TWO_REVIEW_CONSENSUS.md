# Leadership SJT — Two-Review Consensus

Status: METHODOLOGICAL CONSENSUS / PRE-JOB-ANALYSIS
Date: 2026-08-23
Reviewers: Grok + Claude
Branch: hero-webgl

## Shared verdict

Both independent reviewers returned the same high-level verdict:

**C — SUBSTANTIAL COMPETENCY REDESIGN REQUIRED**

Both assigned **78/100 confidence**.

Neither reviewer recommends abandoning SJT methodology. Both recommend revising the competency architecture before writing a new item bank.

## Strong consensus — decisions that can be treated as current working constraints

### 1. Do not preserve the 8+1 architecture

The number of competencies is not a target. The current set has overlap, meta-processes and constructs that are better measured elsewhere.

### 2. Situational Diagnosis is not a standalone headline competency

Both reviewers independently concluded that diagnosis is primarily an upstream process embedded in many scenarios.

Working decision:
- remove it from the primary competency list;
- use it as an item-design constraint and/or response-process evidence;
- optionally test a separate rationale/diagnosis probe later.

### 3. Narrow the V1 target role

Both reviewers reject a single first version covering manufacturing supervisors, project leads, knowledge-work supervisors and other structurally different roles.

Working target:

> Front-line manager with formal people responsibility for a relatively stable operational/service team, reporting upward to another manager.

Project leadership without formal authority and supervision of expert professionals move to future modules.

### 4. Core SJT content should be leaner

Both reviewers converge on these content families as central:
- expectations / direction;
- delegation / autonomy calibration;
- performance accountability;
- conflict/interpersonal judgement;
- prioritisation/decision judgement.

### 5. Ethical / safety / fairness judgement is a major missing area

Both reviewers independently identify this as important, especially for operational front-line leadership.

Working decision:
- add it to Stage 0 job-analysis coding;
- do not assume final domain status until critical-incident evidence confirms it.

### 6. Do not make the SJT carry the whole leadership battery

Both reviewers recommend multi-method assessment.

Likely separation:
- WorkStyle → behavioural preferences / development;
- SJT → situational managerial judgement;
- structured behavioural interview → past behavioural evidence, coaching/development and other constructs where interview is stronger;
- work sample / in-basket → planning, resource allocation, process/system judgement where relevant;
- technical competence → separate role-specific gate.

### 7. WorkStyle and SJT stay independent

Both reviewers support the principle:

`preference ≠ competence ≠ role fit`

But both warn against prematurely turning WorkStyle × SJT combinations into selection rules.

Working decision:
- score independently;
- report independently;
- keep interaction hypotheses in a research register;
- no hard interaction rules before empirical incremental-validity and fairness evidence.

### 8. No legacy stem is reused verbatim for selection

The legacy Situational Leadership items are publicly accessible with scoring logic.

Working decision:
- preserve them as scenario-family / structural references;
- rewrite future selection items from fresh critical incidents;
- no verbatim reuse in a protected assessment bank.

### 9. No leadership percentage / suitable-unsuitable output

Both reviewers reject early use of:
- `78% leadership potential`;
- x/80 style efficiency;
- automatic `suitable / unsuitable` labels;
- hard cut-offs before criterion validation.

## Important reviewer disagreement — keep open until evidence

### A. Content blueprint vs reported measurement model

This is the most important unresolved issue.

**Grok:** recommends a lean multi-dimensional competency evidence profile, with approximately five core competency areas and role modules. Early outputs may remain multidimensional evidence profiles, provided they are not overclaimed.

**Claude:** argues more strongly that several content domains in an SJT do **not** imply several reliable reportable scales. Claude recommends planning for one primary reported score — `Situational managerial judgement (SJT-MJ)` — with only empirically supported facet scores added later.

**Consensus decision:**

Do not decide by preference.

The revised architecture will explicitly distinguish:

1. **CONTENT BLUEPRINT** — domains/scenario families used to design and balance the item bank;
2. **MEASUREMENT MODEL** — the number of scores supported by actual covariance/reliability/criterion data.

Stage 0–4 may track domain-level evidence internally, but no domain is promised as a future reportable score.

### B. Direction/expectation vs accountability

**Grok:** keeps them as separate core competencies, while noting substantial overlap.

**Claude:** recommends merging them into `Expectation and accountability management`.

Decision: test as separate content families during Stage 0 job analysis. Do not settle score structure yet.

### C. Delegation vs coaching/development

**Grok:** retains Delegation as core and treats Coaching as merge/role-conditional/developable.

**Claude:** merges both into `Capability development and autonomy calibration`, while noting structured interview may be the stronger source for coaching evidence.

Decision: retain both as separate incident codes during job analysis, then determine whether they are distinct duties/content families and whether either deserves a separate SJT score.

### D. Change leadership

**Grok:** keeps `Change Implementation Leadership` as a role-conditional competency.

**Claude:** treats change primarily as a **scenario context**, because the behaviours during change largely reuse direction, autonomy, accountability and conflict processes.

Decision: in Stage 0 code both:
- the behavioural competency involved;
- whether the incident occurs under active change.

Only incident evidence determines whether Change emerges as a distinct competency or a context facet.

### E. Operational/systems judgement

**Grok:** role-conditional and potentially SJT-usable.

**Claude:** recommends moving it to job knowledge/work sample because process reasoning may be too role-specific and ability-loaded.

Decision: treat as role-specific until job analysis. Compare SJT vs work-sample method fit after collecting incidents.

### F. Ethical/safety/fairness placement

**Grok:** role-conditional addition, especially for operations.

**Claude:** promotes it to a central fifth domain for the narrowed operational V1 target.

Decision: deliberately oversample safety/ethics/fairness critical incidents during Stage 0 and let frequency + criticality data determine whether it is universal core or operational module.

## Revised provisional content map for Stage 0 — not a measurement model

Use these as **incident coding hypotheses**, not as final scales:

1. Expectation / structure setting
2. Performance accountability
3. Delegation / autonomy calibration
4. Coaching / capability development
5. Conflict / interpersonal judgement
6. Prioritisation / escalation judgement
7. Ethical / safety / fairness judgement
8. Change context (facet, not assumed competency)
9. Operational/process judgement (role-specific candidate)
10. Diagnostic quality (meta-process code, not headline competency)

This deliberately keeps potentially mergeable categories separate during job analysis so the data are not forced into the reviewers' preferred architecture.

## Immediate next gate — Stage 0 Job Analysis

Do **not** write the new SJT bank yet.

Collect critical incidents from the narrowed target role.

Recommended participant structure for a feasible first pass:
- 8–12 experienced front-line managers;
- 5–8 managers-of-managers / direct supervisors;
- ideally 3+ organisations;
- target roughly 40–60 usable critical incidents.

For each incident capture:
- situation/context;
- managerial responsibility;
- what made the situation difficult;
- constraints / competing priorities;
- what the manager did;
- what happened;
- what a weak but realistic response would look like;
- perceived criticality;
- frequency/typicality;
- which provisional content codes apply.

Important: item writing should start **after** incident coding and content coverage review, not before.
