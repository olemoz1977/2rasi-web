# WorkStyle 8-axis — Felt Distinctness Audit

Status: STAGE 0 / RESPONDENT-LEVEL CONSTRUCT AUDIT / PRE-ITEM-WRITING
Date: 2026-08-23

## Purpose

The eight candidate axes may be theoretically distinct while still feeling repetitive to respondents.

This audit asks a stricter question:

> If a respondent had to explain in their own words what each axis is asking, would the eight explanations actually sound different?

A construct should not survive merely because an expert can distinguish it semantically.

---

# 1. Eight different psychological questions

## 1. Structured Preparation ↔ Emergent Organisation

**Felt question:**
> Do I like to create order before I start, or create order while I am already doing the work?

Emotional signature:
**plan first ↔ organise in motion**

Object: work structure.

---

## 2. Method Consistency ↔ Method Adaptation

**Felt question:**
> Once I have a way of doing the work, do I prefer to keep using it or adjust it as I go?

Emotional signature:
**keep the method ↔ tune the method**

Object: work method already in use.

---

## 3. Local / Concrete Focus ↔ System / Relational Focus

**Felt question:**
> When something happens, do I first look closely at the immediate issue or widen the frame and look at connections?

Emotional signature:
**zoom in ↔ zoom out**

Object: level of attention / analysis.

---

## 4. Conclusion Stability ↔ Evidence-led Revision

**Felt question:**
> Once I have reached a conclusion, how readily do new facts make me reopen it?

Emotional signature:
**hold the conclusion ↔ update the conclusion**

Object: conclusion under new evidence.

---

## 5. Opportunity Scanning ↔ Risk Scanning

**Felt question:**
> Faced with an uncertain option, what do I tend to notice first — what might work or what might go wrong?

Emotional signature:
**possibility first ↔ risk first**

Object: attentional orientation under uncertainty.

---

## 6. Autonomous Execution ↔ Collaborative Coordination

**Felt question:**
> When I can either proceed alone or coordinate with others, which route do I naturally prefer?

Emotional signature:
**do independently ↔ coordinate together**

Object: work coordination with others.

---

## 7. Direct Disagreement ↔ Alignment-seeking

**Felt question:**
> When I disagree with someone, do I put the difference directly on the table or first try to reduce friction and find alignment?

Emotional signature:
**surface the disagreement ↔ soften / align first**

Object: interpersonal disagreement.

---

## 8. Expressive Participation ↔ Reserved Participation

**Felt question:**
> In a group, do I naturally take visible conversational space or participate more selectively and quietly?

Emotional signature:
**visible presence ↔ selective presence**

Object: intensity/visibility of participation.

# 3. Highest-risk boundaries

## Boundary A — Method Adaptation vs Conclusion Revision

Risk:
Both can sound like:
> `I change when new information comes.`

Required separation:
- Method Adaptation = **change what I do**;
- Conclusion Revision = **change what I think is true / appropriate**.

Reject any item where both are changed simultaneously.

---

## Boundary B — Conclusion Revision vs Direct Disagreement

Risk:
Both can sound like:
> `I stick to my position or change it.`

Required separation:
- Conclusion Revision = new **evidence** changes my conclusion;
- Direct Disagreement = another **person** disagrees; the question is how openly I handle the difference.

Direct Disagreement must not reward changing one's mind.
Conclusion Revision must not require interpersonal confrontation.

---

## Boundary C — Analysis Level vs Opportunity/Risk Scanning

Risk:
Broad/system thinkers may naturally notice risks and opportunities at wider levels.

Required separation:
- Analysis Level = **how wide is the frame?**
- Opportunity/Risk = **within a comparable frame, which direction gets attention first?**

Opportunity/Risk items should hold information scope roughly constant.

---

## Boundary D — Autonomy/Coordination vs Disagreement vs Participation

Three interpersonal axes can collapse into generic `introverted / collaborative / agreeable` impressions.

Required separation:

### Autonomy/Coordination
No conflict needed.
Question = **Who is involved in doing/deciding?**

### Direct Disagreement/Alignment
Conflict required.
Question = **How is a difference handled?**

### Expressive/Reserved Participation
Neither conflict nor interdependence required.
Question = **How much visible interaction space do I occupy?**

If a future item simultaneously requires teamwork, disagreement and public speaking, it is unusable for construct separation.

# 4. Felt-distinctness verdict by axis

| Axis | Felt distinctness | Main risk | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Structured Preparation ↔ Emergent Organisation | HIGH | overlap with Method Adaptation | KEEP |
| Method Consistency ↔ Method Adaptation | HIGH if execution-stage trigger is explicit | `change` meta-theme | KEEP |
| Local ↔ System Focus | MEDIUM-HIGH | competence + Opportunity/Risk overlap | KEEP / challenge strongly |
| Conclusion Stability ↔ Evidence-led Revision | MEDIUM | resembles Method Adaptation and Disagreement | KEEP ONLY WITH STRICT ITEM GATE |
| Opportunity ↔ Risk Scanning | MEDIUM-HIGH | desirability + system-focus overlap | KEEP / challenge strongly |
| Autonomous ↔ Collaborative | HIGH | can blend with agreeableness | KEEP |
| Direct Disagreement ↔ Alignment-seeking | HIGH if disagreement is explicit | blend with Conclusion Revision | KEEP |
| Expressive ↔ Reserved Participation | HIGH | narrow extraversion | KEEP |

No additional axis is removed at this conceptual stage.

However, **Conclusion Revision remains the most fragile of the eight** because its emotional grammar still resembles `hold ↔ change` and because self-report may reward open-mindedness.

It should be the first axis dropped if cognitive testing cannot make both poles feel legitimate and distinct.

---

# 5. New item-writing prohibition

From this audit onward, every candidate item receives an exclusion check:

1. What is the trigger?
2. What object is being organised / changed / noticed / coordinated?
3. Is another person required?
4. Is disagreement required?
5. Is new evidence required?
6. Is uncertainty required?
7. Which neighbouring axis could this item accidentally measure?

If more than one axis remains equally plausible after answering these, reject the item rather than dual-code it for V1.

This is intentionally stricter than the old 15-dimension architecture.

---

# 6. Current conclusion

The 8-axis architecture is **more respondent-distinct than the 9-axis version**, mainly because removing Commitment Stability eliminates a fourth `maintain ↔ change` dilemma.

The architecture is suitable to move to Stage 0 pole-definition work, but not yet to a full item bank.

Highest-priority challenge axes:
1. Conclusion Stability ↔ Evidence-led Revision;
2. Opportunity ↔ Risk Scanning;
3. Local ↔ System Focus.

The five other axes currently have comparatively clean felt identities.
