# WorkStyle 15 — Bipolar Measurement Architecture Hypothesis

Status: METHODOLOGICAL HYPOTHESIS / NOT ADOPTED
Date: 2026-08-23
Purpose: independent methodological review before any v0.7 item writing

## 1. Why this hypothesis is being considered

WorkStyle 15 currently uses 15 work-style dimensions grouped into five domains, with three items per dimension and a 1–5 workplace-typicality response scale. The current v0.5/v0.6 model treats each dimension as essentially unipolar and uses two direct items plus one reverse-keyed item.

Two internal cognitive pilots produced useful evidence:
- 45 items are substantially more usable than the earlier 75-item form;
- a separate `Sunku įvertinti / neteko tokios situacijos` response exposes ambiguity that would otherwise be hidden in the midpoint;
- several reverse items do not represent a true opposite of the intended construct;
- some reverse items instead measure another legitimate work preference, such as autonomy, local problem focus, stability, explicit-information preference, or risk analysis;
- some items implicitly create a desirable/undesirable direction even though the intended product philosophy is non-evaluative;
- several current dimensions overlap because different constructs are asked through the same failure/problem/change scenario.

This motivates a different hypothesis:

> Some or all WorkStyle dimensions may be better represented as bipolar work-style axes with two legitimate, non-evaluative poles rather than as high/low scores on a desirable trait.

This document does **not** assume that the hypothesis is correct.

---

## 2. Candidate measurement concept

### 2.1 Unit of measurement

Proposed unit:

**one bipolar axis = two legitimate work-style poles**

Example:

`Independent decision-making  ←  neutral zone  →  Collaborative decision-making`

Neither end is defined as better. The question is where a person usually tends to operate in a specified work context.

### 2.2 Candidate response representation

Internal signed coding may be:

- left-most response = -2
- left = -1
- midpoint = 0
- right = +1
- right-most response = +2

The respondent does **not** need to see these numbers.

A separate `Cannot assess / have not encountered this situation` response remains missing data and is not a midpoint.

Important: this is a candidate coding convention, not yet a claim that the five positions form an interval scale. Ordinal modelling should be considered during validation.

### 2.3 Item form

A bipolar item should define both ends of the behavioral choice rather than infer an opposite from low endorsement.

Illustrative form only:

**When I can make a decision on my own...**

`I usually decide independently  ○ ○ ○ ○ ○  I usually involve others`

The two anchors must:
- describe the same behavioral decision space;
- be mutually interpretable as alternatives on one continuum;
- both be psychologically and professionally legitimate;
- avoid a hidden “good” answer;
- avoid asking the respondent to infer the missing opposite.

### 2.4 Midpoint problem

A central response may mean different things:
- both poles are equally characteristic;
- the person genuinely has no directional preference;
- the person behaves differently by context;
- uncertainty.

These meanings must not be silently merged.

A methodological decision is required on whether:
1. the midpoint represents balanced preference only;
2. contextual dependence receives a separate response;
3. uncertainty remains a separate missing response;
4. the instrument captures context sensitivity through repeated scenarios rather than a response option.

## 3. Why “15” is not mathematically problematic

The model does **not** require the 15 current dimensions to be paired with one another.

Fifteen bipolar axes would contain 30 poles:

`15 axes × 2 poles = 30 pole descriptions`

Therefore an odd number of axes is not itself a methodological problem.

The real question is whether each proposed axis is empirically and conceptually bipolar.

## 4. Provisional bipolarity audit of the current 15 constructs

The table below is a hypothesis map, not an accepted architecture. Pole wording is deliberately provisional.

| Current construct | Candidate opposite pole | Initial bipolarity confidence | Main concern |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Goal Persistence | Reprioritisation / switching | Medium | Rational goal abandonment is not necessarily low persistence |
| Work Structure | Improvisation / emergent organisation | High | Likely a natural work-preference continuum |
| Achievement Drive | Sufficiency / completion focus | Medium | “Enough” may reflect efficiency, not low achievement drive |
| Systems Thinking | Local / concrete problem focus | High | Both are legitimate levels of analysis |
| Adaptability | Consistency / stability | High | Strong candidate if anchors avoid “change is always good” |
| Learning Orientation | Proven-method / experience reliance | Medium | Could be partly independent rather than opposite |
| Cooperation | Autonomy | High | Cognitive pilots strongly suggest this distinction |
| Empathic Attention | Explicit / factual cue focus | Medium | A person can be high on both empathy and factual focus |

A full bipolar architecture should **not** be forced if some constructs are better modelled as unipolar or multidimensional.

## 5. Competing architectures that must remain open

Independent review should compare at least four options.

### Model A — Current unipolar architecture
- 15 dimensions;
- typicality/endorsement items;
- direct and reverse items;
- low score means less of the named tendency, not more of an opposite tendency.

### Model B — Fully bipolar architecture
- 15 signed axes;
- both poles explicitly defined;
- no conventional reverse-key items;
- respondent profile is directional rather than high/low.

### Model C — Hybrid architecture
- genuinely bipolar constructs use two-pole items;
- constructs without a defensible opposite remain unipolar;
- different scoring families coexist under one profile.

### Model D — Structural redesign
- do not preserve 15 constructs by assumption;
- use new conceptual and empirical work to reduce, split, rotate, or replace dimensions;
- five current domains may also change or become descriptive groupings rather than scores.

The review should be free to recommend any of these.

## 6. Critical scoring implications

### 6.1 Do not automatically average axes into domain scores

Signed bipolar axes have different semantic directions. A simple mean across three axes is only meaningful if:
- their polarity is intentionally aligned;
- they share a defensible higher-order latent factor;
- empirical data support aggregation.

Until this is demonstrated, the five domains should be treated as **organizational groupings**, not necessarily numerical scores.

### 6.2 Direction and dispersion are different information

With three scenarios on one axis:

`-2, 0, +2` and `0, 0, 0` both have a mean of zero.

They may represent very different response patterns.

Possible research variables:
- **direction**: average signed tendency;
- **within-axis dispersion**: consistency across scenarios.

However, with only three items per axis, dispersion is unstable and must **not** automatically be interpreted as adaptability or contextual flexibility. It may also reflect ambiguity, low reliability, or genuinely different subcontexts.

### 6.3 Missing response

`Cannot assess / have not encountered this situation` remains missing data.

It must not be converted into the scale midpoint.

This is an internal scoring rule and does not need to be foregrounded in respondent-facing copy.

## 7. Theoretical frameworks relevant to the hypothesis

### 7.1 Semantic Differential

The Osgood semantic differential tradition provides a methodological precedent for rating a target between two explicitly defined bipolar anchors. It is relevant to response format and the concept of a meaningful neutral center.

It does **not** by itself prove that the proposed WorkStyle poles are true psychological opposites.

### 7.2 Interpersonal Circumplex

The Interpersonal Circumplex (IPC) is built around two orthogonal bipolar dimensions, commonly described as Agency and Communion. Interpersonal characteristics can be represented as blends of those dimensions.

The IPC is relevant to:
- testing whether some WorkStyle interpersonal constructs occupy a structured two-dimensional space;
- future comparison with DiSC-like circumplex models;
- avoiding arbitrary segmentation of interpersonal constructs.

It does **not** imply that all 15 WorkStyle axes form one circumplex.

### 7.3 Dimensional rather than typological interpretation

The proposed architecture is continuous. It should not collapse people into fixed types.

A profile may show directional preferences on several axes and context-dependent patterns on others.

## 8. Future PAEI and DISC correspondence lenses

PAEI and DISC are **not** part of the WorkStyle core scoring model. They are possible later correspondence layers.

### 8.1 DISC / circumplex-style lens

Everything DiSC publicly describes an eight-scale circumplex supported by a two-dimensional structure, with adjacent scales positively related and opposite scales negatively related.

A future WorkStyle-to-DISC correspondence should therefore not be created by assigning blocks of WorkStyle axes to D, i, S, and C by hand.

More defensible future approach:
1. collect WorkStyle data and an appropriate external benchmark;
2. estimate how WorkStyle axes project into a two-dimensional interpersonal/behavioral space;
3. cross-validate the projection in held-out data;
4. only then consider an external DISC-like ring or lens.

Until validated, any DISC display must be labelled a qualitative correspondence lens, not a derived DISC assessment.

### 8.2 PAEI lens

Adizes PAEI uses four complementary roles: Producer, Administrator, Entrepreneur, Integrator.

These roles should not be assumed to form a zero-sum circumplex.

A future WorkStyle-to-PAEI relationship is better framed as four potentially overlapping projections:

- P = weighted pattern of WorkStyle tendencies;
- A = weighted pattern;
- E = weighted pattern;
- I = weighted pattern.

The four outputs should not automatically be forced to sum to 100%.

Any weights should be theory-informed and then empirically tested, not chosen only because they make the visual look balanced.

### 8.3 “Segments around the radar” risk

A visual outer ring can imply stronger scientific integration than actually exists.

Therefore:
- do not present PAEI/DISC sectors as core WorkStyle dimensions;
- do not imply official equivalence;
- use them only after independent validation or explicitly as exploratory correspondence;
- keep proprietary scoring/item banks separate from WorkStyle.

## 9. Visualization implication

A conventional radar chart assumes that moving outward means “more” of a dimension. This is potentially misleading for bipolar axes because the two directions are qualitatively different, not low versus high.

Candidate visual principles:
- visible neutral baseline;
- equal visual status for both poles;
- no implication that one side is stronger, better, or more developed;
- no 0–100 competence-looking score;
- show pole labels directly;
- separate core WorkStyle profile from optional PAEI/DISC correspondence layers.

Possible formats for later testing:
- signed axis markers around a neutral ring;
- small bipolar bars arranged around a wheel;
- 15 spoke markers with clearly labelled two-sided anchors;
- a non-polygon profile rather than a conventional “bigger area = better” radar.

Visualization should be evaluated after the measurement model, not used to dictate it.

## 10. Proposed validation sequence if bipolar architecture survives review

### Stage 0 — Construct and pole review
For each candidate axis:
- define the latent construct in one sentence;
- define both poles behaviorally;
- explain why the poles are alternatives on the same continuum;
- document foreseeable cases where both poles can be high;
- reject or redesign axes that fail this test.

### Stage 1 — Cognitive item development
For each surviving axis:
- write at least 6–10 candidate scenarios initially;
- include both anchors explicitly;
- test wording with cognitive interviews;
- record ambiguous/context-dependent items;
- reduce to the best items only after evidence.

### Stage 2 — Small pilot
Goals:
- response usability;
- missing-response rate;
- completion time;
- floor/ceiling/end-pole concentration;
- item redundancy;
- whether midpoint use is interpretable.

### Stage 3 — Structural pilot
Prefer a substantially larger sample before claiming dimensional structure.

Analyses to consider:
- polychoric item correlations for ordinal responses;
- exploratory factor analysis / parallel analysis;
- ESEM or ordinal CFA for candidate structure;
- local dependence and item redundancy;
- reliability appropriate to ordinal scales;
- test–retest stability;
- inspection of whether item pairs/scenarios actually behave as one bipolar continuum.

Do not infer “bipolarity” merely because two labels sound opposite in language.

### Stage 4 — External validity
Test expected relationships with independent constructs and/or validated external measures.

Particularly important:
- convergent validity;
- discriminant validity;
- whether interpersonal axes map coherently to Agency/Communion-style space;
- whether any PAEI/DISC correspondence generalises to new participants.

### Stage 5 — Cross-validation
Any mapping weights, cut-points, profile language, or external-lens projections must be tested on data not used to derive them.

### Stage 6 — Language/version invariance
Only after Lithuanian wording stabilises should an English version be developed and measurement invariance examined.

## 11. Evidence status of existing v0.5/v0.6 data

The existing v0.5/v0.6 sessions are useful for:
- cognitive-friction evidence;
- item ambiguity;
- completion time and fatigue;
- identifying failed reverse items;
- identifying construct overlap.

They should **not** be retrospectively rescored as bipolar data.

For example, a response of `1 = not characteristic` to a unipolar statement does not prove endorsement of a specific opposite pole.

A bipolar architecture would begin a new measurement-data series.

---

## 12. Open methodological decisions

Independent review is specifically requested on these questions:

1. Is a bipolar WorkStyle architecture theoretically justified at all?
2. Which of the 15 constructs are defensibly bipolar, and which are not?
3. Should the project prefer fully bipolar, hybrid, or remain unipolar?
4. Is five response positions adequate, or should seven be tested?
5. What exactly should the midpoint mean?
6. Should contextual dependence be a response option, a separate research variable, or inferred from scenario variation?
7. Is three retained items per axis enough for a public reflection tool after a larger candidate bank is validated?
8. What psychometric model is most appropriate for signed ordinal bipolar responses?
9. Should the five domains remain, and if so, as numerical factors or only as conceptual groupings?
10. What evidence would be required before a DISC-like circumplex projection is defensible?
11. What evidence would be required before four PAEI correspondence scores are defensible?
12. Is a radial/radar visualization compatible with bipolar data, or should another geometry be used?
13. What are the largest risks of acquiescence, social desirability, forced trade-off, ipsativity, or artificial opposition in this proposal?
14. Which parts of the current architecture should be abandoned before further item writing?
