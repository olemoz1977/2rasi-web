# WorkStyle construct audit 06 — Intellectual Humility and Social Energy

## Purpose

Evaluate whether `Intellectual Humility` and `Social Energy` survive as WorkStyle constructs, and if so in what form.

No new item bank is written here.

---

## 1. Intellectual Humility / Intelektinis nuolankumas (`HUM`)

Current v0.6 items:
- `HUM-02` Kai suklystu, tai pasakau tiesiai.
- `HUM-04` Kai nežinau, pasakau, kad nežinau.
- `HUM-05` Kai sprendimas jau priimtas, savo nuomonę keičiu retai.

### Current construct problem

The current label combines several morally positive behaviours:
- admitting mistakes;
- admitting uncertainty;
- revising a position.

These are not identical behaviours, and the first two carry a strong social-desirability gradient.

`Humility ↔ conviction` is also a dangerous framing because it contrasts a virtue-labelled pole with a potentially stubborn-looking pole.

### Salvageable work-style signal

The most defensible stylistic signal is not humility itself but **how readily a position is revised when contradictory information appears**.

Working construct:

**Position stability ↔ Position revisability**

or, more neutrally at the latent level:

**Position Revision Threshold**

The axis asks how much counter-evidence / alternative perspective is typically needed before a person changes a settled view.

Both ends can be functional:
- lower revision threshold can support responsiveness and learning;
- higher revision threshold can support consistency, resistance to noise, and commitment.

The axis must avoid:
- `open-minded ↔ stubborn`;
- `humble ↔ arrogant`;
- asking whether the person admits mistakes, which is evaluative rather than stylistic.

### Boundary with Adaptability

This construct is distinct from `Method Consistency ↔ Method Adaptation` if the boundary is maintained:
- Adaptability = changing **how work is done** when circumstances change;
- Position Revision = changing **what one believes / concludes** when evidence changes.

This distinction is conceptually defensible but will require empirical discriminant testing.

### Decision

**RETAIN as a conditional bipolar candidate, fully redesigned.**

Status: CONDITIONAL BIPOLAR.

Do not carry the current HUM items into the new bank.

## 2. Social Energy / Socialinė energija (`ENG`)

Current v0.6 items:
- `ENG-01` Grupės diskusijose dažniausiai aktyviai įsitraukiu.
- `ENG-03` Kai idėja man svarbi, mano entuziazmas dažniausiai matomas.
- `ENG-05` Grupėje dažnai lieku stebėtoju, net kai turiu ką pasakyti.

### Construct clarity

This is one of the cleaner current constructs. It describes how visibly and actively a person tends to occupy social/group interaction space.

A neutral bipolar framing is available:

**Expressive participation ↔ Reserved participation**

or more simply:

**Socially active ↔ Reserved participation**

Both ends can be legitimate:
- active participation can energise discussion and make thinking visible;
- reserved participation can support observation, selectivity, and lower social noise.

### Boundary with Assertiveness

Social Energy must not be confused with Position Assertion.

A person can be:
- socially quiet but very assertive when they speak;
- socially active but highly accommodating;
- socially active and assertive;
- quiet and accommodating.

Therefore `ENG` and `AST` should remain separate candidates.

### Boundary with future DISC lens

This axis is likely to correlate strongly with extraversion / interpersonal activation and therefore may become one of the clearest contributors to a future DISC-like correspondence layer.

That does not make it a DISC score and does not justify hand-mapping it now.

### Decision

**KEEP as a strong bipolar core candidate.**

Working pole pair:

`Expressive participation ↔ Reserved participation`

Status: STRONG BIPOLAR.

## 4. Architecture implication

The interpersonal portion of the reduced core is becoming clearer:

1. `Cooperation ↔ Autonomy` — how work/decisions are socially coordinated;
2. `Position Assertion ↔ Accommodation` — how disagreement / own position is expressed;
3. `Expressive ↔ Reserved Participation` — how much social interaction space is typically occupied;
4. `Position Revision Threshold` — how readily a settled conclusion changes under counter-evidence (conditional candidate).

These are related but describe different behavioural spaces.
