# WorkStyle construct audit 01 — Goal Persistence vs Adaptability

Status: STAGE 0 / CONSTRUCT AUDIT / PRE-ITEM-WRITING
Date: 2026-08-23

## Purpose

Resolve whether the current WorkStyle constructs `Goal Persistence / Kryptingumas` (PER) and `Adaptability / Prisitaikymas` (ADP) should:
- remain separate;
- be merged into one bipolar axis;
- or be redefined into cleaner work-style constructs.

This audit is about construct architecture, not item wording. No v0.7 item bank should be written from this document alone.

---

## 1. What the current v0.6 items actually ask

### PER — Goal Persistence / Kryptingumas

Current v0.6 items:
- PER-02: return to an important task when it takes longer than expected;
- PER-04: try again after an unsuccessful attempt;
- PER-05R: set aside work that has not progressed for a long time and move to something else.

The common behavioural referent is **continuing effort after delay, failure or lack of progress**.

Main problem:
PER-05 does not represent a clean low-persistence pole. Switching away from stalled work may be poor persistence, but it may also be rational reprioritisation. The item does not specify whether new information makes continued effort irrational.

### ADP — Adaptability / Prisitaikymas

Current v0.6 items:
- ADP-01: try another way when the chosen method no longer works;
- ADP-04: reorganise work when priorities change;
- ADP-05R: find it difficult to change a plan once it has been set.

The common behavioural referent is **changing method / plan / work order in response to changed conditions**.

Main problem:
ADP-01 already states that the current method `no longer works`. That makes switching the method normatively correct and risks measuring judgement/competence or socially desirable responding rather than a neutral style preference.

---

## 2. Core distinction: goal level vs means level

The current PER and ADP constructs operate at different levels of control.

### Level A — goal / commitment level

Question:
**How readily do I keep versus reconsider an existing goal or priority when progress is difficult or competing information appears?**

Possible style space:

`commitment stability  ↔  goal / priority reallocation`

This is the conceptual territory currently partly covered by PER.

### Level B — method / strategy level

Question:
**How readily do I keep versus change the method or plan used to pursue the goal when conditions change?**

Possible style space:

`method consistency  ↔  method adaptation`

This is the conceptual territory currently partly covered by ADP.

These are not the same construct.

A person can plausibly show:
- stable commitment to the outcome;
- high willingness to change the method used to reach it.

In practical language: **persistent ends, flexible means**.

Therefore high Goal Persistence and high Adaptability are not logically contradictory.

---

## 3. External theoretical check

Relevant literature on goal regulation distinguishes persistence / goal engagement from goal adjustment processes such as disengagement, reengagement and flexible modification of striving.

A recent meta-analytic review of goal adjustment literature treats goal disengagement, goal reengagement and goal-striving flexibility as related but distinct processes and emphasises their dynamic interplay rather than reducing them to a single persistence continuum.

Research on cognitive control similarly describes a stability–flexibility or shielding–shifting dilemma: stable goal shielding protects ongoing goals from distraction, while flexible control enables updating when conditions change. Both modes have benefits and costs.

Important implication for WorkStyle:
- there is theoretical precedent for a **stability ↔ flexibility** trade-off;
- but this does **not** justify merging every form of persistence and adaptation into one person-level axis;
- the referent must be held constant: goal commitment, method choice, attention switching and task switching are not interchangeable.

Reference anchors used for this audit:
- Goschke and colleagues on cognitive stability / flexibility and the shielding–shifting dilemma;
- Wrosch and colleagues on goal disengagement and reengagement;
- recent meta-analytic work on goal adjustment processes.

---

## 4. Decision on the merge hypothesis

### Decision: DO NOT MERGE PER AND ADP INTO ONE AXIS

Reason 1 — different referents
PER is primarily about persistence of effort / commitment. ADP is primarily about changing method / plan / priority order.

Reason 2 — simultaneous high expression is coherent
A person can strongly protect the goal while changing tactics rapidly. A one-dimensional axis would force this coherent pattern into a false midpoint.

Reason 3 — different failure modes
Too much commitment stability can become perseveration or sunk-cost persistence.
Too much method consistency can become rigidity.
Too much goal reallocation can become unstable commitment.
Too much method adaptation can become unnecessary switching.
These are not the same behavioural errors.

Reason 4 — current items already expose the distinction
PER-05 becomes ambiguous precisely because it shifts from persistence to rational reprioritisation.
ADP items become evaluative when the stem explicitly says the current method no longer works.

## 5. Revised construct hypotheses

### Candidate A — Commitment Stability / Goal Reallocation

Status: **POSSIBLE BIPOLAR — NOT YET ACCEPTED**

Working construct definition:

> The typical threshold for maintaining versus reconsidering commitment to an existing work goal or priority when progress is difficult, competing demands emerge, or new information changes the expected value of continued effort.

Candidate poles:

`Maintain chosen direction  ↔  Reconsider / reallocate direction`

Both poles can be legitimate.

Critical design constraints:
- do not frame one pole as `give up easily`;
- do not frame the other as `never give up`;
- every item must contain a genuine reason why either continued commitment or reconsideration could be sensible;
- distinguish changing the goal from changing the method;
- avoid measuring ability to know the objectively correct decision.

Main unresolved risk:
The threshold may depend so strongly on task value, sunk cost, authority and new evidence that it behaves more like context-sensitive judgement than stable WorkStyle.

This candidate survives only if cognitive testing shows respondents recognise a recurring personal tendency across comparable situations.

### Candidate B — Method Consistency / Method Adaptation

Status: **STRONG BIPOLAR CANDIDATE**

Working construct definition:

> The typical preference for preserving a chosen work method / plan versus modifying the method / plan as conditions and feedback evolve, while the underlying work objective remains broadly stable.

Candidate poles:

`Method consistency  ↔  Method adaptation`

Both poles can be legitimate:
- consistency supports standardisation, learning curves, reliability and disciplined execution;
- adaptation supports responsiveness, experimentation and fit to changing conditions.

Critical design constraints:
- the stem must not say that the current method is already proven to be failing;
- changing priorities and changing methods should not be mixed indiscriminately;
- do not imply `adaptation = good` and `consistency = stubborn`;
- anchor both ends in observable work behaviour.

This is the cleaner successor candidate to current ADP.

---

## 6. Relationship to other WorkStyle constructs

### Work Structure

`Method Consistency ↔ Method Adaptation` must remain distinct from `Structure ↔ Improvisation`.

Possible distinction:
- Structure asks how work is organised before / during execution;
- Method Adaptation asks how readily an already chosen approach is modified in response to evolving conditions.

Empirical overlap is expected and must be tested.

### Learning Orientation / Explore–Exploit

Method adaptation may overlap with the proposed `Explore new methods ↔ Rely on proven methods` axis.

Important distinction to test:
- Learning / Explore–Exploit = where a person tends to source or select methods;
- Method Adaptation = how readily the current method is changed once work is underway.

If respondents do not distinguish these, one construct should be merged or dropped.

### Achievement Drive

Commitment Stability must not become a proxy for `tries hard` or `high standards`. Achievement intensity is a separate hypothesis.

---

## 7. Implications for the reduced WorkStyle architecture

This audit does **not** support reducing 15 to 14 by merging PER + ADP.

Instead, it supports a more precise redesign:

- current PER is **not retained unchanged**;
- current ADP is **not retained unchanged**;
- two cleaner hypotheses remain possible:
  1. Commitment Stability ↔ Goal Reallocation — conditional bipolar;
  2. Method Consistency ↔ Method Adaptation — strong bipolar candidate.

The total construct count remains open. If Candidate A fails cognitive testing as a stable work style, it can be removed without affecting Candidate B.

---

## 8. No new item bank yet

Before writing retained items:

### Candidate A must pass a pole-validity test
Ask whether respondents can describe real work situations in which:
- maintaining direction is sensible;
- reconsidering direction is sensible;
- and nevertheless they recognise a usual personal threshold / tendency.

If answers are almost entirely `it depends on the situation`, Candidate A should not become a scored WorkStyle axis.

### Candidate B must pass a neutrality test
Respondents should view both consistency and adaptation as legitimate styles.

If adaptation is consistently perceived as the obviously competent / desirable answer, the item contexts must be redesigned.

---

## 9. Current decision

**PER + ADP merge hypothesis: REJECTED conceptually for now.**

**Candidate A — Commitment Stability ↔ Goal Reallocation:** retain for Stage 0 / cognitive investigation only.

**Candidate B — Method Consistency ↔ Method Adaptation:** retain as a strong bipolar WorkStyle candidate.

Next construct audit should examine a second likely redundancy cluster rather than writing items immediately.

Recommended next cluster:

`Assertive Communication vs Persuasive Influence`

Reason:
Both independent methodology reviews already flagged likely overlap, and reducing redundant influence constructs is more likely to simplify the architecture than forcing PER and ADP into one axis.
