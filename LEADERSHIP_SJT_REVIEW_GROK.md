## 3. The Meta-Process Problem: Situational Diagnosis

**Situational Diagnosis is not a distinct competency.** It is a general managerial judgement factor / prerequisite cognitive process that is embedded in virtually every scenario.

- A manager who cannot diagnose skill vs motivation vs external obstacle will produce suboptimal responses on Direction, Accountability, Delegation, Coaching, and Change scenarios.
- Treating it as a separate scored competency creates artificial inflation of inter-competency correlations and double-counting of the same underlying reasoning.
- In SJT terms it is better handled as **response-process evidence**: either through a follow-up rationale question (“What was the main reason for your choice?”) or by coding the diagnostic quality of the chosen action itself within the multi-competency vector.

**Scoring implication:** Remove C1 as a primary scale. Retain diagnostic quality as a scored facet or process code that can contribute secondary evidence to other competencies. This reduces construct redundancy and improves interpretability.

## 4. Overlap Analysis

| Pair | Nature of overlap | Acceptable correlated competencies or redundancy? |
|---|---|---|
| Situational Diagnosis vs Priority/Decision | High – both require identifying the critical constraint | Redundancy if Diagnosis is scored separately |
| Direction/Expectation Clarity vs Performance Accountability | Moderate–high – both involve setting standards and follow-up | Acceptable correlation; keep separate but monitor |
| Delegation vs Coaching | High – both involve calibrating support and ownership over time | Partial redundancy; developmental coaching can be folded into Delegation for first-line roles |
| Performance Accountability vs Conflict Judgement | High – many underperformance conversations are interpersonal conflict | Acceptable but high; clear behavioural anchors needed |
| Change Leadership vs Operational/Systems Judgement | Moderate | Acceptable if Change is limited to people-side implementation |
| Priority Judgement vs general cognitive/problem-solving | High | Risk of measuring g rather than managerial judgement; scenarios must force trade-offs specific to people management |

High overlaps indicate that an 8-factor model will struggle to achieve clean discriminant validity. A leaner 5–6 core map is more realistic for a first-line SJT of practical length.

## 5. Missing Competency Audit

The following are sufficiently distinct and job-relevant for many front-line roles and are currently absent or only weakly covered:

- **Ethical / safety / compliance judgement** (especially in manufacturing, healthcare, logistics).
- **Escalation judgement** (when to handle, when to escalate, how to escalate).
- **Fairness / consistency of treatment** (critical for perceived justice and legal risk).
- **Team coordination / interdependence management** (beyond individual performance).
- **Risk judgement under time pressure** (distinct from pure prioritisation).

**Not recommended as separate SJT competencies** (better measured elsewhere):

- Resilience / emotional control → better via interview or observation.
- Learning agility → better via WorkStyle + past behaviour.
- Influence / persuasion as a pure construct → often collapses into Conflict or Direction.

Recommended additions only if SME critical-incident data confirm high criticality: Ethical/Safety Judgement and Escalation Judgement.

## 7. Scenario Design Requirements

- **Source**: Critical-incident interviews with experienced first-line managers and their direct reports in the target roles. Do not start from theory or the legacy S1–S4 key.
- **Contextual information**: Enough to allow realistic diagnosis and trade-offs, but not so much that the “correct” path is telegraphed.
- **Response options**: 4–5 qualitatively different managerial strategies that represent realistic good, mixed, and poor choices. Avoid one obviously virtuous answer and three caricatures. Avoid residual S1–S4 language.
- **Question format**: Prefer “What would you be most likely to do?” or “Which action is most effective?” with partial-credit SME ratings. Ranking or most/least effective formats can be piloted. Avoid pure multiple-response unless carefully constrained.
- **Trade-offs**: Every scenario should force at least one realistic trade-off (time vs quality, support vs accountability, autonomy vs risk, short-term vs long-term).
- **Scenario families**: Balance individual performance, team issues, change/implementation, conflict, prioritisation under constraint, and (where relevant) ethical/safety situations.
- **Specificity vs portability**: Write role-family versions (operations vs knowledge-work) rather than forcing extreme generality. Universal stems with industry-neutral language are acceptable for core items; role modules for high-specificity content.
- **Response time**: Useful only as a secondary flag for extreme rushing or overthinking; do not use as a primary score.

## 8. Scoring Design

The proposed multi-competency evidence vector (one response contributing to 1–3 competencies) is methodologically attractive but high-risk without rigorous SME calibration and later empirical keying.

**Defensible stages**:

- Cognitive prototype: qualitative SME review of stems and options only.
- SME-calibrated pilot: multiple experienced managers rate effectiveness of each option; retain items with adequate consensus; use mean effectiveness ratings or consensus ranks for initial partial credit.
- N≈100 usability pilot: item analysis, option endorsement rates, internal consistency of provisional scales, qualitative feedback.
- N≈300–500: exploratory and confirmatory factor analysis, differential item functioning, reliability, preliminary criterion correlations if concurrent data available.
- Selection use: only after criterion-related validity, adverse-impact analysis, and documented job-analysis linkage.

A single overall “leadership potential” percentage or score should **not** exist at any stage before strong criterion evidence. Early outputs must remain multi-dimensional evidence profiles (stronger / mixed / insufficient / development-risk hypotheses).

## 9. Role-Fit Architecture

One single front-line SJT cannot cleanly cover manufacturing shift supervisors, service team leaders, project leads, and knowledge-work supervisors without substantial construct dilution or irrelevant content.

**Recommendation**: Universal core (Expectation Setting, Delegation, Accountability, Conflict Engagement, Prioritisation) + short role-family modules (operations/process, service/people-intensive, project coordination). Distinguish clearly between managerial judgement (people, priorities, accountability) and job-specific technical competence (the latter belongs outside the Leadership SJT).

## 10. WorkStyle × SJT Relationship

The principle “preference is not competence, and competence is not role fit” is sound and must be preserved.

- Score WorkStyle and SJT completely independently.
- Joint interpretation only after both instruments have independent psychometric evidence and after criterion validation of any interaction hypotheses.
- Do **not** create selection rules of the form “strong autonomy preference + weak delegation = risk” until empirical data demonstrate incremental predictive validity and fairness. Premature interaction rules risk double-counting personality and creating adverse impact.

## 11. Selection-Validity and Fairness Gate

Before any real hiring or promotion use the following must be documented:

- Job analysis (task and KSAO) linking each retained competency to critical work behaviours for the specific role family.
- Content validity evidence (SME ratings of relevance and representativeness).
- Adequate SME agreement on scoring keys.
- Reliability (internal consistency and, ideally, test–retest or alternate-form).
- Criterion-related validity (concurrent or predictive) against relevant performance criteria.
- Incremental validity over structured interview and other existing predictors.
- Subgroup fairness and adverse-impact monitoring.
- Accessibility and language equivalence evidence.
- Item security and coaching/retest protocols.
- Human review and explainability of individual score profiles.
- Governance appropriate to high-stakes personnel decisions (documentation, appeal process, ongoing monitoring).

Until these exist, the instrument remains a research or development tool only.

## 12. Practical Validation Sequence (Small Independent Project)

1. **Stage 0 – Construct & job analysis** (current): Critical-incident interviews + 5–8 experienced managers. Pass criterion: clear behavioural anchors and consensus on core vs peripheral constructs. Claims: none beyond “plausible hypothesis.”
2. **Stage 1 – Content & SME keying**: 8–12 SMEs rate relevance and effectiveness of draft items/options. Pass: adequate inter-rater agreement; items with low consensus revised or dropped. Claims: content validity only.
3. **Stage 2 – Usability pilot** (N≈60–100 mixed experience managers): Item analysis, qualitative feedback, provisional scale reliabilities. Pass: acceptable endorsement rates, no major comprehension failures, α ≥ .70 on provisional scales. Claims: usability and internal structure hypotheses.
4. **STOP / REDESIGN GATE**: If Stage 1–2 show persistent high inter-competency correlations, poor SME consensus, or weak face validity for the target roles → redesign competency map and item bank before larger investment.
5. **Stage 3 – Structural validation** (N≈300+): Factor structure, DIF, reliability. Concurrent criterion data if available. Claims: structural validity and preliminary criterion relations.
6. **Stage 4 – Predictive / incremental validation** in operational settings before any selection cut scores.

### 4. Top 5 strengths of the proposal
1. Explicit separation of WorkStyle (preference) from SJT (judgement).
2. Rejection of a single overall leadership percentage score in early stages.
3. Recognition that the legacy tool’s value lies in the scenario stems, not the S1–S4 scoring.
4. Multi-competency evidence coding concept (when properly calibrated).
5. Clear statement that the model is Stage 0 / unvalidated.

### 5. Top 5 methodological risks
1. Treating Situational Diagnosis as a separate competency rather than a meta-process.
2. High construct overlap producing non-discriminant scales.
3. Attempting to measure too many constructs via SJT alone.
4. Premature interaction rules between WorkStyle and SJT.
5. Insufficient role specificity for a coherent first-line target population.

### 6. Best measurement method by competency
See Method-Fit Audit table above. Core SJT constructs: Expectation Setting, Delegation, Accountability, Conflict Engagement, Prioritisation. Everything else primarily interview, work sample, or role-specific methods.

### 7. Recommended SJT response format
4–5 realistic managerial action options per scenario. “Most effective” or “most likely to do” stem. SME partial-credit ratings. Optional rationale probe on a subset of high-value items. No residual S1–S4 framing.

### 8. Recommended scoring architecture
Multi-dimensional evidence profiles only in early stages. SME consensus or mean effectiveness ratings for partial credit. Later empirical keying and latent-trait modelling once sample sizes permit. No composite “leadership potential” score until criterion validity is established.

### 9. Validation roadmap
See Practical Validation Sequence above, with explicit STOP/REDESIGN gate after SME keying and small usability pilot.

### 10. WorkStyle integration verdict
Keep fully independent. Joint interpretation only after separate validation of both instruments and empirical demonstration of any interaction hypotheses. No hard selection rules based on combinations until then.

### 11. Selection-use gate
Documented job analysis, content validity, SME agreement, reliability, criterion-related and incremental validity, fairness analyses, accessibility evidence, item security, and appropriate high-stakes governance must all be in place. Until then the tool is research/development only.

### 12. GO / REVISE / STOP before writing the new scenario bank
**REVISE.**  
Do not write a new item bank against the current 8+1 hypothesis. First complete critical-incident job analysis and SME consensus on a leaner, non-redundant competency map (recommended 5 core + role modules). Only then proceed to scenario writing.
