# WorkStyle 15 — two-review consensus

Status: PRE-PILOT REVIEW CONSENSUS
Last updated: 2026-08-23

## Reviewers

Two independent blind reviews were run against the same WorkStyle 15 v0.1 review package without showing either reviewer the other's conclusions.

Reviewer A (Claude):
- verdict: **READY AFTER MINOR REVISIONS**;
- confidence: **72/100**;
- overall construct-overlap judgment: **Normal**;
- provenance reveal did not change the verdict.

Reviewer B (Grok):
- verdict: **READY AFTER MINOR REVISIONS**;
- confidence: **78/100**;
- overall overlap judgment: normal/expected for a workplace self-reflection instrument.

Gemini was considered as an additional reviewer but did not complete the review because it could not reliably read the supplied review file. Its non-review is not counted as evidence.

## Strong convergence

Both completed reviews independently support:
- retaining the 5 domains × 3 dimensions architecture for pilot;
- no major structural redesign before data;
- five items per dimension as a defensible pilot starting point;
- equal weighting before empirical evidence exists;
- theoretical 0–100 indexes only, not percentiles or norms;
- the non-evaluative interpretation philosophy (`high != good`, `low != bad`);
- showing the 15 dimensions before the five domain summaries;
- PAEI and DISC as optional qualitative correspondence lenses only;
- no numeric PAEI/DISC derived scoring in the first pilot;
- empirical monitoring of neighbouring constructs rather than merging them by theory alone.

Both highlighted these boundary questions for empirical testing:
1. Achievement Drive vs Goal Persistence.
2. Social Energy vs Persuasive Influence / Assertive Communication.
3. Constructive Outlook vs Emotional Regulation.

The reviews also identified reflection/learning overlap as worth monitoring, although they emphasized slightly different item-level mechanisms.

## Important disagreement / uncertainty

### Social Energy
Claude rated Social Energy **Weak** and raised the possibility that it is narrow-band extraversion or a moderator rather than a standalone scale.

Grok rated Social Energy **Acceptable**, judged the Influence domain coherent, and recommended empirical testing rather than structural removal.

Decision: **KEEP FOR PILOT / TEST DISCRIMINANT VALUE**.

Do not remove or merge Social Energy before response data exists. The disagreement itself makes it an ideal empirical question.

### Reverse-item ratio
Claude recommended moving toward two reverse items per dimension.

Grok explicitly praised the systematic one-reverse-per-dimension design and found 14/15 reverse items acceptable, with only PER-05 needing polishing.

Decision: **DO NOT FORCE A 2:3 R/D RATIO**.

Retain one natural reverse item per dimension for the pilot. Examine direct/reverse method effects empirically before adding more reverse wording.

### Factor-analysis recommendations
Both reviewers correctly identify factor structure as an empirical question, but advanced CFA/bifactor comparisons require an adequate sample.

Decision:
- cognitive/early pilot: comprehension, distributions, ceiling/floor effects, corrected item-total patterns, inter-scale correlations, completion time, abandonment, comments;
- larger sample later: EFA/CFA/model comparisons if sample size and data quality justify them;
- do not label small-sample factor analyses as validation.

## Accepted second-review item refinements

Grok independently flagged four low-risk wording improvements not fully resolved in v0.2:
- PER-05: remove the two-behaviour construction;
- SYS-01: reduce double-barrel wording;
- INF-01: replace an ability claim with behavioural wording;
- REF-01: reduce triple-barrel wording.

These refinements are incorporated in `WORKSTYLE15_V03_LT_ITEMS.md`.

## Overall decision

The two completed independent blind reviews provide sufficient conceptual review coverage before a human cognitive pilot.

Current architecture decision: **PROCEED**.

No third AI reviewer is required before pilot. Additional value should now come primarily from real respondent behaviour and cognitive debriefing rather than additional model opinions.
