# WorkStyle 15 — independent blind review brief

Status: READY FOR EXTERNAL REVIEW
Last updated: 2026-08-23

## Purpose

This brief is for an independent reviewer who has **not** participated in designing WorkStyle 15.

The review should evaluate the current model on its own merits rather than endorse prior design decisions.

## Materials to review

Primary:
- `WORKSTYLE15_V01_LT_ITEMS.md`
- `WORKSTYLE15_INTERPRETATIONS_LT.md`
- `WORKSTYLE15_PAEI_DISC_CROSSWALK.md`

Optional only after the first-pass verdict:
- `WORKSTYLE15_INDEPENDENT_MODEL_SPEC.md`
- `WORKSTYLE15_DECISIONS.md`

## Blind-review rule

For the **first pass**, do not provide the reviewer with:
- the history of the legacy ADEPT-like workbook;
- the prior 150-item source;
- earlier assistant conclusions;
- the reason specific dimensions were chosen;
- the intended answer that the architecture should be accepted.

The reviewer should first infer what the instrument appears to measure from the current 75 items and result model alone.

## Reviewer role

Act as a critical psychometric / organizational-behaviour reviewer of a pre-pilot workplace self-reflection instrument.

Do not assume the proposed structure is correct.
Do not optimize for politeness or agreement.
Distinguish clearly between:
- construct logic;
- item quality;
- user-experience usefulness;
- psychometric claims that cannot yet be supported without pilot data.

## Questions to answer

### 1. Architecture
Evaluate whether the proposed **5 domains × 3 dimensions** structure is coherent.

For each domain, answer:
- Do the three dimensions form a meaningful higher-order workplace domain?
- Is any dimension placed in the wrong domain?
- Are any two dimensions too similar to justify separate scales?
- Is there an important workplace construct missing?

### 2. Dimension distinctness
For all 15 dimensions, rate conceptual distinctness as:
- Strong
- Acceptable
- Weak

Identify likely cross-loading pairs and explain why.

### 3. Item audit
Review all 75 items.

Flag items that are:
- double-barrelled;
- vague;
- overly socially desirable;
- identity labels rather than behaviours;
- too context-specific;
- likely to cross-load onto a neighbouring dimension;
- semantically redundant;
- awkward or ambiguous in Lithuanian;
- poor reverse-key items.

For every flagged item, provide:
- item ID;
- problem;
- suggested rewrite or recommendation to drop.

### 4. Reverse-key quality
Assess whether the 15 reverse-key items:
- are natural rather than artificially negative;
- measure the opposite pole of the intended construct;
- introduce avoidable confusion;
- are likely to produce method effects.

Recommend KEEP / REWRITE / DROP for each reverse item.

### 5. Response scale and scoring
Evaluate the proposed 1–5 Likert design and transparent dimension index.

Comment on:
- whether 5 items per dimension is a defensible pilot starting point;
- whether equal weighting is reasonable before evidence exists;
- what should and should not be claimed from a 0–100 theoretical scale index;
- what pilot statistics should determine later shortening or weighting.

### 6. Result interpretation
Review the 15 dimension interpretations and 5 domain summaries.

Check whether they:
- avoid “high = good / low = bad” framing;
- accurately reflect the item content;
- contain Barnum-style generic statements;
- overstate what a self-report instrument can infer;
- provide genuinely useful reflection prompts.

### 7. PAEI and DISC lenses
Treat PAEI and DISC as optional interpretive crosswalks, not independent tests.

Evaluate:
- whether the current Primary / Secondary mappings are conceptually defensible;
- where mappings are forced or misleading;
- whether any numeric derived score should be avoided in the first pilot;
- whether the lenses add explanatory value or mainly visual novelty.

Do not assess official Adizes or DISC validity; assess only whether the proposed WorkStyle correspondence is honest and useful.

### 8. Independence / overlap
Without being told the model’s historical origin, identify frameworks or established constructs that the current WorkStyle dimensions strongly resemble.

State whether this is:
- normal conceptual overlap;
- excessive structural imitation;
- impossible to judge from the materials.

### 9. Pilot readiness verdict
Choose exactly one:
- **READY FOR COGNITIVE PILOT**
- **READY AFTER MINOR REVISIONS**
- **NEEDS MAJOR REVISION BEFORE PILOT**
- **REJECT CURRENT ARCHITECTURE**

Then give:
- confidence 0–100%;
- the 5 strongest parts of the design;
- the 10 highest-priority changes;
- what must be tested empirically rather than debated theoretically.

## Required output format

1. Executive verdict — max 300 words.
2. Domain-level assessment table.
3. 15-dimension distinctness table.
4. Item-level flags table.
5. Reverse-item verdict table.
6. PAEI/DISC lens assessment.
7. Pilot-readiness verdict.
8. Prioritized change list.

## Second-pass reveal

Only after the reviewer has committed to the first-pass assessment may they be shown the historical/provenance notes in `WORKSTYLE15_DECISIONS.md`.

Then ask one final question:

> Does learning the model history materially change your earlier verdict? If yes, specify exactly what changes and why. If no, state why the first-pass evaluation still stands.

This two-pass sequence is intended to reduce confirmation bias and prevent the reviewer from merely validating the design story already written by the project team.
