**R01 — Clay vessel support vs. shaping**

1. Most important difference: A is *stabilizing/protecting* an existing form (preventing deformation); B is *actively shaping/refining* the form (improving evenness and symmetry).
2. Preference for A: someone who values care, protection, low-risk handling, or is more comfortable with a supportive/custodial role.
3. Preference for B: someone drawn to visible skill demonstration, active control, or the satisfaction of improving something.
4. B reads as slightly more "skilled" or "competent" looking, since precise hand pressure and symmetry are explicitly called out as visible and relevant — A's task doesn't have an analogous visible skill marker (a cradle can be secured with lower apparent precision).
5. Meaningful: the active-shaping vs. passive-stabilizing distinction is core. Nuisance advantage: B's explicit mention of "quality of hand pressure and symmetry is visibly relevant" gives it an implied skill/competence cue not matched by anything in A — this looks like an asymmetry outside the intended contrast (assuming the contrast is meant to be about support vs. shaping, not skill level).
6. A viewer would need to understand that both are legitimate care-taking or working actions on unfinished pottery — that neither is "the point where the pot becomes good/bad," just two different modes of engaging with an in-process object.
7. Verdict: **AMBIGUOUS** (directionally clean concept, but B carries a stronger implicit skill/competence cue than A).

**R02 — Rod alignment vs. inspection window**

1. Most important difference: A performs a *physical correction* (adjusting a block so the rod can pass); B performs a *revealing/observing* action (sliding a cover to expose existing state) with no change to the mechanism.
2. Preference for A: someone drawn to fixing, active intervention, tangible progress/completion (rod moving forward).
3. Preference for B: someone drawn to transparency, verification, or low-intervention/observational approaches.
4. A is more "consequential" in a narrow sense — it enables continued physical progress (the rod advancing), which could read as more "useful" or goal-completing than B's static reveal. B does not advance anything.
5. Meaningful: fixing/enabling motion vs. revealing/observing state is the intended contrast. Nuisance advantage: A's action visibly produces forward progress (rod continuing through the path) while B's produces no change at all — this makes A look inherently more "productive" or "impactful," which may be an artifact of the setup rather than a deliberate part of the contrast.
6. A viewer needs to assume that "seeing clearly" and "aligning/fixing" are being treated as equally valid types of contribution, even though one visibly changes the physical outcome and the other doesn't.
7. Verdict: **NONSEMANTIC_DOMINATED** (A's "progress" cue outweighs the intended align-vs-reveal contrast).

**R03 — Simultaneous exchange vs. independent joint-fitting**

1. Most important difference: A is a *reciprocal, interpersonal* action (mutual give-and-take between two people); B is *independent parallel* work (each person separately executing a precise task in their own fixture).
2. Preference for A: someone who values cooperation, mutual exchange, or relational/social framing.
3. Preference for B: someone who values individual craftsmanship, precision, or self-contained competence.
4. B implies a visible technical skill marker ("flush and square," "attention is on fit and execution quality") that A lacks — A's action (passing pieces) requires no comparable precision or skill demonstration.
5. Meaningful: relational/reciprocal vs. independent/parallel is the intended contrast. Nuisance advantage: B again carries an explicit competence/precision cue not mirrored in A, similar to R01's pattern.
6. A viewer needs to assume that exchanging objects and fitting joints are treated as comparably "meaningful" actions, despite one requiring visible skill and the other not.
7. Verdict: **AMBIGUOUS** (clean interpersonal-vs-individual contrast conceptually, but skill-cue asymmetry favors B).

**8. Recurring idea across pairs**

Across R01-B, R02-A, and R03-B, there's a recurring pattern where one variant per pair involves a *precise, skill-demonstrating physical adjustment with a visible correctness criterion* (symmetry, alignment, flush/square fit), while the paired alternative (R01-A, R02-B, R03-A) involves *supporting, observing, or reciprocating* — actions with no analogous visible "getting it right" standard. This isn't confined to one pair; it shows up three times with different objects, tools, and social contexts (solo pottery, mechanical rod, two-person joinery).

**9. Is this broader than a narrow "careful hands / dexterity" trope?**

Partially, but not fully. R01 and R03 both feature hands executing a precision-fit or precision-form task, which does resemble a dexterity/craft trope. R02, however, replaces a person's dexterous partner-action with a mechanical alignment task — no hands-based skill display is emphasized beyond "small positional adjustment." The recurring idea is closer to "one option has a visible correctness/quality standard the other option structurally cannot have" (a nuisance dominance issue), rather than a single visual trope like manual dexterity. The craft/dexterity flavor is present in R01 and R03 but not clearly in R02, so the recurring pattern is better described as *asymmetric visibility of a quality/correctness criterion* rather than a specific trope like hand skill.
