## R01

**1. Most important meaningful difference between A and B:**
In A, one participant's action changes the routing/allocation of a shared central system, while the others keep interacting independently. In B, all three are equally engaged in a symmetric, closed-loop reciprocal exchange with no state change to the system.

**2. What might make a person prefer A:**
The visible effect on the shared system's routing might read as more consequential or interesting; the asymmetry of one mover among two continuers might draw attention to that action.

**3. What might make a person prefer B:**
The symmetric, reciprocal, face-to-face exchange might read as more socially engaging or balanced; equal attention distribution might feel more inclusive.

**4. Obvious dominance (competence/safety/completeness/attractiveness/power/usefulness/salience):**
No. Both show three equally postured, equally lit participants. A has one differentiated action (route change) which may carry slightly more visual salience due to the state change, but nothing marks either as more competent, safe, or attractive.

**5. Hidden story/assumption needed to understand the contrast:**
A viewer would need to know that "changing route/allocation of a central system" is meant to represent something different in kind from "reciprocal exchange with no state change" — i.e., that the study cares about a distinction between altering a shared system's outcome versus mutual/reciprocal interaction with no systemic change.

**6. Overall verdict:** AMBIGUOUS — the difference is legible but the significance of "route change" vs. "closed-loop exchange" is not self-evident without an interpretive frame.

## R02

**1. Most important meaningful difference between A and B:**
In A, the hand performs a precision/fine-tolerance correction to a moving part with no downstream consequence (both paths stay available/unchanged). In B, the hand performs a low-skill selection that changes which downstream path the flow follows, with no change in tolerance/precision.

**2. What might make a person prefer A:**
The precision framing (gauge, tolerance, careful adjustment) might read as more careful, skilled, or meticulous.

**3. What might make a person prefer B:**
The visible consequence (the flow's course actually changes) might read as more impactful or decisive, despite requiring less skill.

**4. Obvious dominance:**
Partially yes on one axis: A is explicitly described as requiring more skill/precision ("precision adjustment wheel," "narrow physical tolerance") while B is explicitly "low-skill." This is a stated asymmetry in skill level, not just an inferred one. However, on consequence/impact, B is dominant (it visibly changes the system's course; A does not). So the two variants are dominant on different, non-overlapping axes — neither is dominant overall, but each has a named advantage on a different dimension.

**5. Hidden story/assumption needed:**
A viewer would need to assume the study is contrasting "skill/precision without consequence" against "low skill with consequence" — i.e., that skill and consequence are being deliberately decoupled and that this decoupling is the point of interest.

**6. Overall verdict:** NONSEMANTIC_DOMINATED — the explicit "low-skill" vs. "precision" language is a stated, visible asymmetry that could dominate preference independent of any intended construct, since one variant is textually flagged as requiring less skill.

## R03

In A, the focal participant's adjustment changes a shared routing element affecting which station gets the next item (external/shared consequence). In B, the focal participant adjusts only their own workstation configuration, affecting only their own next task (self-contained consequence); others are unaffected either way.

**2. What might make a person prefer A:**
The shared/systemic effect might read as more consequential or central to the group's outcome.

**3. What might make a person prefer B:**
Self-contained control over one's own tools/setup might read as more autonomous, low-risk, or personally relevant.

**4. Obvious dominance:**
No stated asymmetry in competence, safety, attractiveness, or power. Both are described as "visibly legitimate" or unremarkable adjustments. The other two participants are equally active/non-subordinate in both.

**5. Hidden story/assumption needed:**
A viewer would need to assume the point of interest is whether an action's consequence extends to the shared system/other stations (A) versus stays confined to the actor's own task (B) — i.e., a distinction between external-reaching versus self-contained agency.

**6. Overall verdict:** CLEAN_CONTRAST — the difference (shared/external routing effect vs. self-contained configuration effect) is clearly and symmetrically stated with no confounding skill, status, or salience asymmetries.
