# PrioLens gap-family visual viability probe v0.1

Status: ACTIVE CONCEPT PROBE / NO IMAGE GENERATION YET
Date: 2026-08-31

## Rule

This is deliberately practical.

For each underrepresented impulse family, ask:

> Can two materially different, vivid everyday scenes make the intended pull plausible within about one second, without text and without a paragraph of hidden story?

We do **not** require semantic purity. We do require that the intended pull is more than a researcher explanation pasted onto a generic scene.

No images are generated in this step.

## 1. CARE / CAREGIVING

Working pull:
> `I want to protect, support or give something for another's welfare.`

### Mechanism A — giving cover at own cost

Scene logic:
- two people in rain;
- one deliberately holds the umbrella mainly over the other person;
- the helper's own shoulder/body remains visibly exposed.

First-read target:
- protecting / caring for the other;
- mild visible self-cost makes this different from simple togetherness.

Risks:
- rain/protection cue may dominate and collapse into SAFETY/PROTECTION;
- interpersonal warmth can dominate.

### Mechanism B — tending another person's immediate need

Scene logic:
- one person wraps a blanket around another visibly cold person, or supports an injured/tired person;
- action is one-way rather than reciprocal.

First-read target:
- helping / caring / tending.

Risks:
- illness/injury can create negative salience;
- could become generic emergency assistance.

### Verdict

**HIGH VISUAL VIABILITY.**

Care is a real Gallery gap, not an inherently abstract construct. It deserves visual prototyping later.

## 2. RECOGNITION / ESTEEM

Working pull:
> `I want my contribution or standing to be seen and acknowledged.`

### Mechanism A — visible social acknowledgement

Scene logic:
- one person stands beside a completed contribution/object;
- a small group directs clear appreciative attention toward that person: applause, congratulatory gesture, handshake or raised thumbs;
- no trophy or podium required.

First-read target:
- being acknowledged by others.

Risks:
- can collapse into CONNECTION if social warmth dominates;
- can collapse into SUCCESS if the completed outcome/trophy dominates.

### Mechanism B — singled out for acknowledgement within a peer group

Scene logic:
- several peers stand together;
- one peer is clearly being congratulated/acknowledged by the others;
- visual emphasis is the group's response, not superior clothing, wealth or rank.

First-read target:
- being seen / appreciated / recognized.

Risks:
- status and belonging are hard to separate;
- applause is a strong social-reward cue.

### Verdict

**MODERATE-HIGH VISUAL VIABILITY.**

Recognition is visualizable, but its clean distinction from Success and Belonging must be tested rather than assumed.

---

## 3. ACHIEVEMENT / SUCCESS

Working pull:
> `I want to reach the result, win or meet the standard.`

### Mechanism A — crossing a clear finish

Scene logic:
- runner/participant visibly breaks a finish tape or reaches an obvious endpoint;
- the defining feature is completion of a goal, not applause or a trophy.

First-read target:
- achieving / finishing / winning.

Risks:
- sports-specific familiarity and action salience;
- may read as speed/competition rather than broader success.

### Mechanism B — objective outcome just reached

Scene logic:
- a difficult construction/task has visibly reached its finished state while the actor is still present;
- the outcome is obvious without checklist text or symbolic badges.

Possible everyday families:
- final piece locks into a large assembly;
- tall structure becomes stable/complete;
- target quantity physically reaches a marked endpoint without numerical text.

First-read target:
- result achieved.

Risks:
- completion may dominate without the stronger achievement meaning;
- skillful assembly may collapse into MASTERY.

### Verdict

**HIGH VISUAL VIABILITY FOR SUCCESS/GOAL ATTAINMENT, LOWER FOR GENERAL `ACHIEVEMENT`.**

Use concrete goal attainment first. Do not inflate it into a broad achievement trait.

---

## 4. AUTHENTICITY / SELF-EXPRESSION

Working pull:
> `I want to express or preserve what feels genuinely mine.`

### Mechanism A — personalizing a generic object

Scene logic:
- person actively makes an otherwise generic personal object distinctly their own: clothing, bag, workspace item, instrument, etc.;
- emphasis is on self-expression, not technical craft quality.

First-read target:
- personal expression / making it mine.

Risks:
- may read as creativity, decorating or customization;
- aesthetics can dominate choice.

### Mechanism B — choosing own expression against a visible default

Scene logic:
- a set of near-identical default options/context is visible;
- person chooses or wears a personally distinctive alternative without exaggerated color/salience.

First-read target:
- choosing what fits me rather than conforming.

Risks:
- likely collapses into AUTONOMY/choice;
- distinctive item can win simply through novelty/salience.

### Verdict

**BORDERLINE VISUAL VIABILITY.**

Self-expression is visualizable; `authenticity` as an inner alignment claim is much harder. If tested visually, name the candidate family **SELF-EXPRESSION**, not Authenticity. Keep broader authenticity in the reflection layer unless evidence supports more.

## 5. CONTRIBUTION / SERVICE

Working pull:
> `I want what I do to benefit something beyond myself.`

### Mechanism A — replenishing a shared resource

Scene logic:
- one person adds/replenishes something clearly used by several others;
- beneficiaries or shared-use structure are visible in the same frame.

Possible everyday families:
- refilling a communal water station while multiple cups/users are visible;
- restocking a shared resource point that others are actively using.

First-read target:
- contributing to a shared good.

Risks:
- can collapse into wider-system INFLUENCE;
- can look like routine work/service occupation.

### Mechanism B — repairing something for communal use

Scene logic:
- one person repairs/restores a clearly shared object/space while others' use/benefit is visible or imminent in the same frame.

First-read target:
- making something better for others / shared use.

Risks:
- repair skill can dominate as MASTERY;
- benefit to others can still depend on inferred story.

### Verdict

**MODERATE VISUAL VIABILITY.**

Contribution is worth a targeted prototype, but it may remain inseparable from Care or Influence in static imagery. Do not force a separate family unless choices/reasons distinguish it.

---

## 6. IMMERSION / ENGAGEMENT

Working pull:
> `I want to be absorbed in an activity.`

### Mechanism A — deep task absorption

Scene logic:
- person is visibly engrossed in a demanding activity with peripheral distractions present but ignored;
- no performance result is shown.

First-read target:
- being absorbed / focused.

Risks:
- a still image can only depict concentration, not the subjective experience of flow;
- may collapse into work, MASTERY or effort.

### Mechanism B — expressive activity with sustained attention

Scene logic:
- musician, dancer, maker or reader deeply engaged mid-activity;
- no audience, reward, finished output or competitive goal.

First-read target:
- engagement for its own sake.

Risks:
- activity-specific attractiveness can dominate;
- skill/aesthetic cues remain strong.

### Verdict

**LOW-MODERATE VISUAL VIABILITY.**

A static image can plausibly show **ENGAGEMENT/FOCUS**, but not confidently `immersion/flow`. If retained visually, narrow the label to ENGAGEMENT. Broader immersion belongs in reflection until evidence says otherwise.

## Probe result

### Ready for later visual prototype

1. **Care / caregiving** — high viability.
2. **Recognition / acknowledgement** — moderate-high viability.
3. **Success / goal attainment** — high viability.

### Worth one constrained prototype, but narrow the construct

4. **Self-expression** — use instead of broad Authenticity.
5. **Contribution / shared benefit** — moderate viability; likely neighbour leakage.
6. **Engagement / focus** — use instead of broad Immersion.

### Keep outside visual channel for now

- Justice / fairness;
- Purpose / meaning;
- broad Authenticity;
- broad Immersion.

## Important product implication

The visual and reflection channels do **not** need identical category lists.

That asymmetry is a feature if it prevents weak imagery:

- visual channel asks what can be revealed by vivid comparative scenes;
- perceived-sufficiency channel can cover broader needs/values that people can meaningfully self-assess but that do not survive one-frame visual operationalization.

The two channels should later be compared at a **macro-domain or carefully mapped family level**, not forced into one-to-one item equivalence.
