# PrioLens INF cross-edge identity concepts v0.1

Status: TEXT-ONLY GATE D1 / NO ASSETS
Date: 2026-08-30

## Purpose

Test whether INF · Influence / Poveikis can retain one coherent meaning across materially different opponents and visual mechanisms.

This is not a stimulus showcase and not evidence that INF is a valid construct.

Working INF definition:

> meaningful impact on the direction, decision or outcome of an external/shared situation.

Gate question:

> Does the INF-side difference remain interpretable as changing an external/shared course or outcome when the opponent changes, rather than becoming scale, dominance, generic causality or scene-specific utility?

## Non-negotiable constraints

Within each pair:
- one shared scene/domain;
- same people and status cues where people appear;
- same camera, lighting and object family;
- both variants functionally legitimate;
- no text, labels, arrows or explanatory symbols;
- no good/bad asymmetry;
- no `more/bigger/busier` shortcut for INF;
- no boss/controller vs passive-followers shortcut for INF;
- INF must be carried by a visible change in course/distribution/shared state.

Across the three pairs:
- use materially different scene mechanisms;
- do not reuse one visual grammar three times;
- do not assume that success in one edge transfers to another.

## I1 · MAS ↔ INF
### Mechanism class: precision vs routing direction

Shared scene:
A neutral tabletop mechanical flow apparatus on a workshop bench. One hand is visible. The apparatus contains the same central mechanism and two equally ordinary downstream paths. Same amount of material/tokens is present in both variants. No people in the background, no industrial status cues, no dramatic machinery.

MAS-side candidate:
The hand uses a small precision adjustment wheel and caliper-like gauge to align one central moving part to a narrow physical tolerance. Both downstream paths remain available and visually unchanged. The dominant visible action is fine correction/precision.

INF-side candidate:
The hand uses one simple low-skill selector on the same central mechanism to change which of the two equal downstream paths the same existing flow follows. Total amount, speed, number of destinations and visual density remain unchanged. The dominant visible consequence is that the course of the external system changes.

Why this is useful:
- INF is expressed as **directional leverage**, not larger magnitude;
- MAS is expressed as precision/quality, not technical-domain prestige;
- both sides operate on the same apparatus.

Failure modes:
- INF may still read as generic mechanical control;
- MAS may read as maintenance rather than mastery;
- the fine-tool visual may be more visually complex than the selector.

Pass signal:
Blind reasons for the INF side repeatedly refer to changing where the system goes, directing the outcome, redirecting the process, deciding the route, or equivalent language rather than `bigger effect`.

## I2 · AUT ↔ INF
### Mechanism class: self-configuration vs shared process configuration

Shared scene:
A neutral workshop room with three equal workstations and three adults in ordinary identical-status clothing. All three remain active in both variants. Camera, people, positions, lighting and object counts are fixed. No supervisor position, uniforms, podiums, pointing gestures or passive observers.

AUT-side candidate:
The focal participant adjusts only their own modular workstation surface/tool arrangement between two visibly legitimate configurations. The two other participants continue independently at their unchanged stations. The adjustment changes how the focal participant can perform their own next task, but does not alter the shared material route or the other stations.

INF-side candidate:
The focal participant makes one simple adjustment to a central neutral routing element located between all three stations. The same amount of shared material remains in the system, but the adjustment visibly changes which station receives the next item / which route the shared item follows. The other two participants remain equally active and visually non-subordinate.

Why this is useful:
- AUT is not represented by `many arbitrary options`; it is meaningful control over one's own method/setup;
- INF is represented by changing a shared process course;
- status asymmetry is deliberately suppressed.

Failure modes:
- `own workstation` still requires some ownership inference;
- INF may be interpreted as logistics/efficiency rather than influence;
- focal-person salience could itself create agency bias.

Pass signal:
Blind reasons distinguish `I can set up/do it my way` from `this changes what happens in the shared process / where the shared outcome goes` without invoking leadership or power over people.

## I3 · CON ↔ INF
### Mechanism class: reciprocal relation vs consequence on shared state

Shared scene:
Three adults sit equally spaced around a round neutral table. Same people, clothing, facial visibility, posture range and lighting in both variants. In the center is one simple physical tabletop system with three identical participant-side interaction points. No business setting, no head of table, no audience and no passive participant.

CON-side candidate:
All three participants are engaged in a reciprocal hand-to-hand exchange through the same central object: each passes/receives one identical neutral piece in a closed loop. Attention and body orientation are distributed toward one another. The central system state itself does not change route or allocation.

INF-side candidate:
All three participants remain active at equal interaction points around the same tabletop system. One participant moves one of three identical controls; that visible adjustment changes the route/allocation of the same central neutral piece within the shared tabletop system. The other participants continue interacting with their equal controls rather than watching the focal person.

Why this is useful:
- faces, people count and social salience are matched;
- CON is expressed through reciprocity/mutual exchange;
- INF is expressed through consequence on shared state, not rank or passive followers.

Failure modes:
- CON may collapse to cooperation/task coordination;
- INF may collapse to puzzle/mechanical causality;
- both sides may be read as teamwork if the central apparatus dominates.

Pass signal:
Blind reasons for CON emphasize mutual exchange/contact/reciprocity, while INF reasons emphasize altering the shared arrangement/course/outcome, without `leader`, `boss`, `controls others` or dominance language.

## Cross-edge identity criterion

The three local pairs are not enough merely to be interpretable.

INF survives Gate D1 only if blind reasons across I1, I2 and I3 share a coherent semantic core such as:
- changing the course of something beyond the actor;
- redirecting an external/shared process;
- altering what happens to a shared system/outcome;
- consequential leverage on direction/allocation.

INF does **not** survive if the three pairs instead produce unrelated dominant explanations such as:
- I1 = mechanical switch preference;
- I2 = leadership / authority / being in charge;
- I3 = puzzle solving / coordination;

or if INF is consistently explained by visual magnitude, activity level, number of affected objects or attractiveness.

## Decision rule before rendering

Run blind text-concept review first.

Reviewer Stage 1 must not see:
- INF/AUT/MAS/CON labels;
- intended mappings;
- the cross-edge identity hypothesis.

For each pair ask:
1. What is the most important meaningful difference between A and B?
2. What might make a person prefer A?
3. What might make a person prefer B?
4. Is either side obviously more competent, safe, complete, attractive or powerful?
5. What hidden story is required?

Only after responses are locked reveal the intended mappings and ask whether the three intended INF sides share one coherent concept stronger than their local mechanisms.

## Current recommendation

I1 is the cleanest concept candidate.
I2 is harder but strategically necessary because AUT is relational and historically weak.
I3 remains the hardest because CON and INF both naturally recruit social/coordination meaning.

Do not render all three until blind concept review finds no fatal text-level confound.
