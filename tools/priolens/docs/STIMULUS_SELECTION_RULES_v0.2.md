# PrioLens stimulus selection rules v0.2

Status: ACTIVE
Updated: 2026-08-31

## Product principle

> **For Fun with Wisdom**

PrioLens is not a laboratory image-isolation exercise. A useful stimulus does not need to encode one perfectly sterile visual mechanism.

The practical question is:

> Can a person understand the intended semantic idea quickly and with little ambiguity, while no obvious visual nuisance dominates the choice?

## Active selection rule

A standalone image is worth carrying forward when it satisfies all three:

1. **Fast semantic readability**
   - within roughly a second, a reasonable viewer can say what is happening or what the scene represents;
   - no long backstory or designer explanation is required.

2. **Semantic fit is stronger than alternatives**
   - the intended candidate idea is a natural first reading;
   - alternative readings may exist, but should not dominate.

3. **No gross preference distortion**
   - avoid extreme differences in threat, attractiveness, luxury/status, food reward, face salience, brightness/saturation, visual complexity or spectacle when these could swamp the intended comparison.

This is a screening rule, not validation.

## What is no longer required

Do not reject an otherwise useful stimulus merely because:
- it contains more than one visible cue;
- the scene is not a pure one-mechanism manipulation;
- an indicator visibly reacts to an adjustment;
- the image can be described at more than one semantic level;
- a broad direction is not psychologically isolated by the image alone.

The prior over-sterile rule produced unnecessary complexity and pushed PrioLens toward artificial laboratory scenes.

## Candidate directions

The original eight directions remain useful **design hypotheses**:
- AUT — autonomy / self-directed choice or control
- CER — certainty / clarity / settled state
- EXP — exploration / discovery
- MAS — mastery / competence / quality execution
- CON — connection / reciprocal relation
- INF — influence / meaningful impact on a wider environment
- PRO — protection / shielding / safeguarding
- OPP — opportunity / available valuable possibility

They are not validated scores and do not need equal quotas by force.

The D1/D2 negative evidence still matters: broad direction aggregation cannot be assumed merely because the designer labels several images the same way. Therefore build multiple materially different exemplars and let later evidence decide whether a direction survives.

## Lower-level F1-F8 work

F1-F8 and related local-mechanism labels remain useful as diagnostic notes and failure-analysis vocabulary. They are **not the active product taxonomy**.

Use them only when they help explain why a stimulus fails or why two images may be semantically different despite sharing an old broad label.

## Practical bank-building rule

For each promising broad direction:
- collect roughly 4-6 materially different standalone scenes;
- prefer semantic diversity over cosmetic variations;
- do not create matched A/B mates;
- remove obvious duplicates and extreme nuisance outliers;
- use blind AI only as a cheap description/confound filter;
- later use humans to determine which images actually map reliably enough to support a direction.

The image bank should be broad enough to let bad exemplars fail without forcing the direction to fail immediately.

## AI screening

Blind AI review should answer simple questions such as:
- What is visibly happening?
- What are the strongest 2-3 semantic readings?
- Is the intended candidate direction among the natural readings?
- What visual feature might dominate preference?

Do not run long consensus loops or treat AI agreement as construct validation.

## Example from current work

A hand turning a physical audio volume control with a visible level response is acceptable as a candidate for local/self-directed control if the first reading is simply "adjusting the volume".

It should not be rejected solely because the level indicator also shows a system response.

## Guardrails

- one image = one standalone stimulus;
- two images shown together are a comparison event, not a matched pair;
- no mandatory explanation during the public flow;
- no personality/diagnosis/hidden-self claims;
- D1/D2 negative evidence is retained;
- broad direction labels remain hypotheses until later evidence supports them;
- avoid methodological perfectionism that makes the stimuli artificial or the product slow.
