# PrioLens Open14 repeated construct audit

Status: RESEARCH AUDIT / NO RUNTIME CHANGE / TAXONOMY NOT VALIDATED
Date: 2026-09-05
Repository: `olemoz1977/2rasi-web`
Branch: `feature/priolens-architecture`

## Purpose

Re-audit the current PrioLens architecture after the owner explicitly challenged the assumption that:
- 14 Channel-A visual families are established human need dimensions;
- 12 Channel-B items are an established 12-need taxonomy;
- the two channels should map one-to-one.

This audit treats the current bank and Channel-B items as formative product constructs and checks them against established motivational psychology, affective/cognitive neuroscience, social neuroscience, curiosity/information-seeking research, sense-of-agency research, meaning/beneficence research, visual-attention theory, and best-worst scaling methodology.

This document does **not** validate any PrioLens construct. It identifies stronger and weaker theoretical anchors and the empirical work still required.

## Current measurement boundary

### Channel A

Current protocol:
- 14 visual families;
- each family appears 3 times through 3 unique exemplars;
- participant selects MOST = first visual pull;
- when MOST is clear, participant also selects LEAST = lower pull among the two remaining images;
- MOST and LEAST are stored and interpreted separately;
- no MOST-minus-LEAST psychological score;
- repeated family selection across exemplars is formative evidence only.

### Channel B

Current protocol:
- 12 self-report statements;
- respondent rates **current perceived sufficiency**, not importance;
- 12 items are grouped into 6 product-facing domains.

### Critical ontology warning

The 14 A families and 12 B items are **not all the same type of construct**.

Across the current architecture there is a mixture of:
- biological/homeostatic requirements;
- deficit/defensive motives;
- basic psychological needs in the SDT sense;
- social reward/status processes;
- caregiving/prosocial action systems;
- curiosity/information-seeking systems;
- sense-of-agency/control processes;
- eudaimonic meaning/beneficence constructs;
- environmental affordances/opportunities.

Therefore the scientifically safest umbrella terms are:
- Channel A: **visual attention directions / visual mechanisms**;
- Channel B: **current sufficiency areas / sufficiency statements**.

Do not call all 14 A families or all 12 B items “basic human needs”.

## Evidence framework

### 1. Self-Determination Theory / Basic Psychological Need Theory

Strongest established psychological-need anchors:
- autonomy;
- competence;
- relatedness.

Safety/security is treated in SDT as a strong deficit/defensive motive rather than one of the three basic psychological needs.

References:
- Ryan RM, Deci EL. Self-Determination Theory, 2017; current overview 2022.
- Vansteenkiste M, Ryan RM, Soenens B. Basic psychological need theory: Advancements, critical themes, and future directions. Motivation and Emotion. 2020. DOI: 10.1007/s11031-019-09818-1.
- Reeve J, Lee W. A neuroscientific perspective on basic psychological needs. Journal of Personality. 2019. PMID: 29626342.

### 2. Affective neuroscience

Pankseppian affective neuroscience supports primary systems such as SEEKING, CARE, FEAR, PLAY, etc. These systems are not equivalent to PrioLens need items, but they provide biological plausibility for:
- exploratory/seeking behavior;
- caregiving;
- defensive/threat behavior.

References:
- Davis KL, Montag C. Selected Principles of Pankseppian Affective Neuroscience. Front Neurosci. 2019. PMID: 30705615.
- Panksepp J. Affective neuroscience of the emotional BrainMind. Dialogues Clin Neurosci. 2010.

### 3. Social affiliation

Relatedness/affiliation has strong psychological and neural support. Acute social isolation can evoke social craving responses analogous in some respects to hunger.

Reference:
- Tomova L et al. Acute social isolation evokes midbrain craving responses similar to hunger. Nature Neuroscience. 2020. DOI: 10.1038/s41593-020-00742-z.

### 4. Curiosity and information seeking

Curiosity, exploration and information seeking are motivationally meaningful and engage reward/value systems. The literature distinguishes, but does not cleanly separate in all paradigms:
- novelty/exploration;
- desire to know;
- information seeking;
- learning.

References:
- Kobayashi K et al. Neural mechanisms of information seeking. Neuron. 2024.
- Poli F et al. Curiosity and the dynamics of optimal exploration. Trends Cogn Sci. 2024.

### 5. Sense of agency / control

Sense of agency is the experience of being the author of an action and exerting control over its effects. It is related to but conceptually distinct from autonomy/volition.

References:
- Kaiser J et al. The Interplay Between Affective Processing and Sense of Agency During Action Regulation. Front Psychol. 2021. DOI: 10.3389/fpsyg.2021.716220.
- Moccia L et al. Sense of Agency and Its Disturbances. Psychiatry Clin Neurosci. 2024. DOI: 10.1111/pcn.13601.

### 6. Meaning and beneficence

Meaning in life is a serious and well-studied well-being construct, commonly decomposed into:
- coherence/comprehension;
- purpose;
- significance/mattering.

This does not establish meaning as one additional universal “basic psychological need”.

Beneficence/contribution predicts well-being, but evidence reviewed within SDT suggests it may be better considered a strong wellness enhancer than a basic psychological need on the same footing as autonomy, competence and relatedness.

References:
- King LA, Hicks JA. The Science of Meaning in Life. Annual Review of Psychology. 2021. DOI: 10.1146/annurev-psych-072420-122921.
- Martela F, Steger MF. The three meanings of meaning in life. J Pos Psychol. 2016. DOI: 10.1080/17439760.2015.1137623.
- Martela F, Ryan RM. Distinguishing between basic psychological needs and basic wellness enhancers: the case of beneficence. Motivation and Emotion. 2020. DOI: 10.1007/s11031-019-09800-x.

### 7. Receiving versus giving care

Giving support and receiving support are not interchangeable. Social neuroscience explicitly distinguishes them, and they can show different neural associations.

References:
- Inagaki TK, Eisenberger NI. The neurobiology of giving versus receiving support. Psychosom Med. 2016. PMID: 26867078.
- Inagaki TK, Eisenberger NI. An empirical review of the neural underpinnings of receiving and giving social support. Psychosom Med. 2013. PMID: 23804014.
- Rigney N, Hong W. Prosocial Helping Behavior: Conceptual Issues and Neural Mechanisms. Biological Psychiatry. 2025. DOI: 10.1016/j.biopsych.2025.03.003.

### 8. Visual attention

First visual pull is not a pure readout of motive or need. Contemporary attention models emphasize at least:
- physical salience;
- current goals;
- selection history.

Therefore stimulus construction and nuisance-factor control are central to Channel-A validity.

References:
- Failing M, Theeuwes J. Selection history: How reward modulates selectivity of visual attention. Psychonomic Bulletin & Review. 2018. DOI: 10.3758/s13423-017-1380-y.
- Theeuwes J. Visual Selection: Usually Fast and Automatic; Seldom Slow and Volitional. Journal of Cognition. 2018.
- Theeuwes J. Attentional Capture and Control. Annual Review of Psychology. 2025.

### 9. MOST–LEAST / Best-Worst Scaling analogy

The PrioLens procedure has a formal resemblance to object-case Best-Worst Scaling:
- repeated short choice sets;
- one most and one least selection;
- forced trade-offs;
- each object/family appears repeatedly.

But PrioLens must not claim standard BWS/MaxDiff validity automatically because:
- the latent criterion is “first visual pull”, not conventional preference/importance;
- the alternatives are images standing in for constructs;
- exemplar nuisance effects can dominate;
- MOST and LEAST may not be inverse manifestations of one latent utility.

BWS literature explicitly notes that combining best and worst is safer when the inverse relationship holds; otherwise separate models can predict better.

References:
- Cheung KL et al. Experimental measurement of preferences using best-worst scaling: an overview. Health Econ Rev. 2016. PMCID: PMC4705077.
- Flynn TN et al. Best-worst scaling: what it can do and how to do it. J Health Econ. 2007. PMID: 16707175.
- Louviere JJ, Flynn TN, Marley AA. Best-Worst Scaling. Cambridge University Press. 2015.
- Hollis G, Westbury C. When is best-worst best? Behav Res Methods. 2018. PMID: 29322399.

### A-family structural conclusion

The current 14 can remain useful as **visual families**, but the audit does **not** support presenting them as 14 equivalent motivational needs.

More defensible higher-order interpretation:

1. Restoration/homeostasis: REST
2. Resource availability: RESOURCE
3. Defense/safety: SAFETY
4. Structure/predictability cue: ORDER
5. Affiliation/relatedness: CONNECTION + BELONGING
6. Caregiving/prosocial action: CARE
7. Volition/autonomy: AUTONOMY
8. Agency/action-effect control: CONTROL
9. Social approval/recognition: RECOGNITION
10. Competence/mastery: MASTERY
11. Curiosity/information seeking: EXPLORATION + KNOWLEDGE
12. Environmental affordance/opportunity: OPPORTUNITY

This is not a proposed final 12-family bank. It is an ontology audit showing that some of the current 14 are subfamilies of broader systems.

## MOST–LEAST methodological audit

### What the current design gets right

1. **Forced trade-off**
   - Relative choice is more discriminating than asking participants to rate all attractive images highly.
2. **Repeated exposure by family**
   - Each family appears three times through distinct exemplars.
3. **No exact image repeat**
   - reduces trivial image-memory repetition.
4. **MOST and LEAST remain separate**
   - this is the correct conservative choice.
5. **No MOST-minus-LEAST psychological score**
   - keep this prohibition.
6. **A+ stays analytically separate**
   - correct; it must not alter raw MOST counts.

### What the design does NOT establish

Repeated MOST or LEAST does not by itself show:
- a need;
- an unmet need;
- stable trait strength;
- subconscious truth;
- construct validity.

### Specific MOST–LEAST risk

Standard BWS assumes that “best” and “worst” choices can often be modeled as opposite information on a common latent preference scale.

PrioLens has a harder problem:
- MOST = first visual pull;
- LEAST = lower pull among remaining images.

The inverse relationship is an **empirical question**, not an assumption.

Therefore:
- analyze MOST and LEAST separately first;
- test whether family-level MOST and LEAST effects are empirically inverse;
- only if supported should any combined choice model be considered for research, never participant-facing scoring.

### Visual nuisance factors to model

At minimum:
- screen slot / position;
- exemplar identity;
- human presence / face count;
- social scene versus object scene;
- luminance/contrast;
- color saturation;
- visual complexity;
- positive/negative valence;
- action clarity;
- novelty/unusualness;
- text/symbol presence;
- apparent reward/food/wealth cues.

Attention theory predicts that physical salience, current goals and selection history can all affect first visual selection.

## Recommended pilot analysis

### Family-level choice models

Run MOST and LEAST separately.

Preferred first-pass model:
- conditional/multinomial logit or mixed-effects choice model;
- participant random effect;
- family effect;
- exemplar effect;
- slot effect;
- trial/order effect.

Then test:
1. does a family effect survive exemplar controls?
2. are the three exemplars directionally coherent?
3. are MOST and LEAST family parameters approximately inverse?
4. do particular exemplars dominate their family?
5. do social/human scenes systematically outperform object scenes?
6. do high-valence families dominate MOST or low-valence families dominate LEAST?

### Self-explanation as construct evidence

The current post-focus question can provide formative convergent evidence.

Code participant explanations against:
- intended family mechanism;
- neighboring family mechanism;
- generic aesthetic attraction;
- personal memory;
- unclear/other.

A family should not be promoted merely because it gets repeated MOST. Promotion should require:
- cross-exemplar repetition;
- no single-exemplar dominance;
- manageable nuisance bias;
- participant explanations that often match the intended mechanism;
- acceptable separation from neighboring families.

## Decision recommendations

### KEEP now

Do not rebuild the current v0.4 runtime based only on theory review.

Keep the 14-family formative bank for data collection/testing, but change the scientific language around it:
- “14 visual directions/families”, not “14 needs”.
- “12 sufficiency areas/statements”, not “validated 12 human needs”.

### REWORK conceptually before external validation

Priority 1:
- RESOURCE: remove “reward” from the construct definition; test/replace reward-heavy exemplars if needed.
- ORDER: downgrade A↔B mapping from DIRECT to RELATED.
- OPPORTUNITY: treat as affordance/environmental possibility, not internal need.
- CARE: preserve as caregiving/prosocial direction; never equate with received support.
- AUTONOMY_AGENCY B ID: separate autonomy wording from sense-of-agency/control terminology.

Priority 2:
- explicitly model CONNECTION and BELONGING as two visual subfamilies of broader relatedness unless data show reliable separation;
- explicitly model EXPLORATION and KNOWLEDGE as two visual subfamilies of broader curiosity/information-seeking unless data show reliable separation.

### Product taxonomy

The existing 6×2 Channel-B grouping may remain as a **product navigation taxonomy**.

It must not be described as a neuroscience-derived six-level or twelve-need architecture.

## Bottom-line audit verdict

1. The current PrioLens system has several strong theoretical anchors, but **not 14 validated needs and not 12 validated needs**.
2. Channel A is best understood as a heterogeneous bank of **visual motivational/action mechanisms**.
3. Channel B is best understood as **12 current sufficiency areas**, spanning biological, contextual, psychological, social and eudaimonic domains.
4. The strongest direct scientific anchors are:
   - rest/restoration;
   - safety/defense;
   - relatedness;
   - autonomy;
   - competence/mastery;
   - curiosity/information seeking.
5. CARE, CONTROL and OPPORTUNITY are not failures. They are evidence that Channel A includes mechanisms that do not have direct Channel-B counterparts.
6. Meaning, contribution and received support are evidence in the opposite direction: Channel B contains important self-report domains without direct A equivalents.
7. This asymmetry supports the frozen principle that Channel A and Channel B are **independent perspectives**, not one latent scale.
8. MOST–LEAST is methodologically promising as relative choice data, but it does not validate the constructs. The critical next evidence must come from pilot choice modeling, exemplar controls and participant self-explanations.
