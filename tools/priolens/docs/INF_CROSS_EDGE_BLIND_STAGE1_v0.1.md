## Reviewer instruction

You are reviewing three candidate visual A/B pairs for a behavioral choice study.

Do not assume any psychological model or hidden construct.

For each pair answer only from the visible concept description:

1. What is the most important meaningful difference between A and B?
2. What might make a person prefer A?
3. What might make a person prefer B?
4. Is either side obviously more competent, safe, complete, attractive, powerful, useful or visually salient?
5. What hidden story or assumption would a viewer need in order to understand the contrast?
6. Overall verdict: CLEAN_CONTRAST / AMBIGUOUS / NONSEMANTIC_DOMINATED / INFEASIBLE_AS_MATCHED_PAIR.

Then, after all three are reviewed, answer:

7. Do any variants across different pairs appear to express a recurring broader idea despite using different scenes? If yes, describe that recurring idea in your own words without inventing labels.

Do not infer intended mappings. Do not try to be helpful to the designer.

In the center is one simple physical tabletop system with three identical participant-side interaction points. No business setting, no head of table, no audience and no passive participant.

Variant A:

One participant moves one of three identical controls; that visible adjustment changes the route/allocation of the same central neutral piece within the shared tabletop system. The other participants continue interacting with their equal controls rather than watching the focal person.

All three participants are engaged in a reciprocal hand-to-hand exchange through the same central object: each passes/receives one identical neutral piece in a closed loop. Attention and body orientation are distributed toward one another. The central system state itself does not change route or allocation.

## R02

Shared scene:
A neutral tabletop mechanical flow apparatus on a workshop bench. One hand is visible. The apparatus contains the same central mechanism and two equally ordinary downstream paths. Same amount of material/tokens is present in both variants. No people in the background, no industrial status cues, no dramatic machinery.

Variant A:
The hand uses a small precision adjustment wheel and caliper-like gauge to align one central moving part to a narrow physical tolerance. Both downstream paths remain available and visually unchanged. The dominant visible action is fine correction/precision.

Variant B:
The hand uses one simple low-skill selector on the same central mechanism to change which of the two equal downstream paths the same existing flow follows. Total amount, speed, number of destinations and visual density remain unchanged. The dominant visible consequence is that the course of the external system changes.

## R03

Shared scene:
A neutral workshop room with three equal workstations and three adults in ordinary identical-status clothing. All three remain active in both variants. Camera, people, positions, lighting and object counts are fixed. No supervisor position, uniforms, podiums, pointing gestures or passive observers.

Variant A:
The focal participant makes one simple adjustment to a central neutral routing element located between all three stations. The same amount of shared material remains in the system, but the adjustment visibly changes which station receives the next item / which route the shared item follows. The other two participants remain equally active and visually non-subordinate.

Variant B:
The focal participant adjusts only their own modular workstation surface/tool arrangement between two visibly legitimate configurations. The two other participants continue independently at their unchanged stations. The adjustment changes how the focal participant can perform their own next task, but does not alter the shared material route or the other stations.
