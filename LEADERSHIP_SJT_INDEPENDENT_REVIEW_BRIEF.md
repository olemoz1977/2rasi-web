# Leadership SJT — Independent Methodological Review Brief

Status: REVIEW BRIEF / BLIND CHALLENGE
Date: 2026-08-23

## Purpose

Perform an adversarial independent methodological review of the proposed Leadership SJT V1 competency architecture before any new situational judgement item bank is written.

Treat the architecture as an **unvalidated hypothesis**. Do not improve or defend it by default.

The reviewer may recommend:
- retaining the proposed architecture;
- merging competencies;
- splitting competencies;
- removing competencies;
- adding missing competencies;
- changing the target role;
- abandoning the proposed SJT architecture if the construct model is not defensible.

Do **not** assume that 8 core competencies + 1 role-conditional competency is the correct number.

# Working product architecture under review

`WorkStyle → Leadership SJT → Role Profile`

Interpretation layers such as PAEI and DISC are explicitly outside the competency model and must not be used to justify competency definitions.

Target role for V1:

**Front-line / operational manager**

Examples:
- team leader;
- shift supervisor;
- production supervisor;
- operational/project lead with direct people responsibility.

The review must evaluate whether this target population is sufficiently coherent for one common SJT.

---

# Proposed competency candidates

Core:

1. Situational diagnosis
2. Direction and expectation clarity
3. Delegation and autonomy calibration
4. Performance accountability
5. Coaching and development judgement
6. Interpersonal and conflict judgement
7. Change and uncertainty leadership
8. Priority and decision judgement

Role-conditional:

9. Operational / systems judgement

---

# Reviewer stance

Use psychometric, personnel-selection, SJT, construct-validity and job-analysis principles.

Challenge the proposal especially where:
- a competency is actually a process that underlies several other competencies;
- two competencies may be behaviourally inseparable;
- a competency is better measured by work sample, interview, cognitive assessment or other method rather than SJT;
- a competency cannot reasonably be inferred from a short situational judgement response;
- competency definitions mix ability, knowledge, personality, preference and managerial judgement;
- the same response could receive different scores depending on unstated context;
- scoring depends too heavily on one leadership theory;
- the proposed model could generate unfair or misleading selection decisions.

Do not use the instrument creator's personal work history, personality or pilot profile as validity evidence.

# Required review tasks

## 1. Architecture verdict

Choose one primary verdict:

A. **PROCEED WITH CURRENT 8+1 ARCHITECTURE**

B. **PROCEED AFTER MINOR MERGES / REFINEMENTS**

C. **SUBSTANTIAL COMPETENCY REDESIGN REQUIRED**

D. **SJT IS NOT THE RIGHT PRIMARY METHOD FOR THIS PURPOSE**

Give confidence 0–100 and name the main source of uncertainty.

---

## 2. Competency-by-competency audit

For all nine candidates provide a table with:

- competency;
- construct clarity;
- behavioural separability;
- relevance to first-line management;
- suitability for SJT measurement;
- overlap with other competencies;
- whether it is must-have at entry or realistically developable;
- verdict: KEEP / MERGE / SPLIT / DROP / ROLE-CONDITIONAL;
- recommended replacement if needed.

Do not preserve the proposed names if better construct definitions exist.

---

## 3. Test the meta-process problem

Specifically evaluate whether **Situational Diagnosis** is:

- a distinct competency;
- a general managerial judgement factor;
- a prerequisite process embedded in most scenarios;
- or something that should be scored as response-process evidence rather than a separate competency.

Explain how this choice would affect SJT scoring.

---

## 4. Test likely overlaps

Explicitly examine at least:

- Situational Diagnosis vs Priority / Decision Judgement;
- Direction / Expectation Clarity vs Performance Accountability;
- Delegation vs Coaching;
- Performance Accountability vs Conflict Judgement;
- Change Leadership vs Operational / Systems Judgement;
- Priority Judgement vs general cognitive/problem-solving ability.

State which overlaps are acceptable correlated competencies and which indicate redundancy.

---

## 5. Missing competency audit

Do not assume the proposed set is comprehensive.

Check whether first-line managerial selection requires separate coverage of concepts such as:

- ethical / safety judgement;
- conflict engagement;
- decision quality under pressure;
- planning / coordination;
- resource allocation;
- communication clarity;
- influence;
- resilience / emotional control;
- learning agility;
- fairness / consistency;
- risk judgement;
- team coordination;
- escalation judgement.

Recommend additions only where they are sufficiently distinct and job-relevant.

---

## 6. Method-fit audit

For every retained competency identify the best primary evidence source:

- SJT;
- WorkStyle self-report;
- structured behavioural interview;
- work sample / simulation;
- cognitive/problem-solving measure;
- past-performance evidence;
- 360 / observer rating;
- other.

A competency does **not** need to stay inside the SJT simply because it matters for leadership.

The goal is a defensible selection battery, not a maximal SJT.

---

## 7. Scenario design requirements

Recommend how future scenarios should be built.

Address:

- critical-incident sourcing;
- amount of contextual information;
- avoiding one obviously virtuous answer;
- realistic trade-offs;
- whether to ask "most likely", "best action", ranking, rating or multiple-response questions;
- whether response options should represent qualitatively different strategies rather than S1–S4 styles;
- scenario-family balance;
- content specificity vs cross-industry portability;
- role level;
- response-time usefulness, if any.

---

## 8. Scoring design

Evaluate the proposed idea that one response may create evidence on 1–3 competencies.

Discuss alternatives:

- keyed best-answer scoring;
- partial-credit scoring;
- SME effectiveness ratings;
- multidimensional response vectors;
- consensus / empirical keying;
- latent-trait modelling after sufficient data.

State what is defensible at:

- cognitive prototype stage;
- SME-calibrated pilot stage;
- N≈100 usability pilot;
- N≈300–500 structural validation stage;
- later selection use.

Explicitly address whether a single overall "leadership potential" score should exist at all.

---

## 9. Role-fit architecture

Evaluate whether one front-line-manager SJT can reasonably cover:

- manufacturing shift supervisor;
- service team leader;
- project lead;
- office/knowledge-work supervisor.

If not, recommend:

- universal core + role modules;
- separate role families;
- adaptive scenario routing;
- another architecture.

Distinguish **managerial competence** from **job-specific technical competence**.

---

## 10. WorkStyle × SJT relationship

Evaluate the principle:

> preference is not competence, and competence is not role fit.

Assess whether WorkStyle and SJT should be:

- scored independently;
- interpreted jointly only after validation;
- used for interaction hypotheses;
- or kept completely separate in selection decisions.

Identify specific risks of creating rules such as:

`strong autonomy preference + weak delegation judgement = managerial risk`

before criterion validation.

---

## 11. Selection-validity and fairness gate

Describe what evidence would be required before using the SJT in real manager selection.

Address:

- job-analysis evidence;
- content validity;
- inter-rater / SME agreement;
- reliability / information precision;
- criterion-related validity;
- incremental validity over interview / work sample;
- subgroup fairness and adverse-impact monitoring;
- accessibility / language effects;
- retest / coaching effects;
- security and item leakage;
- human review and explainability.

Do not provide legal advice, but flag where high-stakes personnel-selection governance is required.

---

## 12. Practical validation sequence

Propose a staged plan realistic for a small independent project.

For each stage provide:

- purpose;
- recommended participant type;
- approximate sample size;
- analysis method;
- pass/fail criterion;
- what claims become defensible;
- what claims remain prohibited.

Include an explicit **STOP / REDESIGN gate** before expensive large-sample validation.
