## Strongest methodological warnings

1. Semantic/linguistic opposites do not prove structural bipolarity.
2. Declaring both poles neutral does not remove social desirability.
3. Two-anchor semantic differentials can create a semi-ipsative forced trade-off when both tendencies may coexist.
4. Three ordinal items per axis are too weak for structural claims or individual-level precision unless reduced from a larger validated candidate bank.
5. The number 15 is inherited rather than empirically derived.
6. Midpoint can confound balance, low endorsement of both, indifference, and context-dependence.

## Architecture recommendation

**Hybrid, reduced target: approximately 9–11 constructs rather than preserving 15 by default.**

Suggested core:
- Bipolar: Work Structure, Adaptability/Consistency, Cooperation/Autonomy, Assertive/Accommodating, Social Energy/Reserved.
- Conditional bipolar: Goal Persistence, Explore/Exploit, Position Revision, Opportunity/Risk scanning.
- Unipolar: Achievement Drive, Empathic Attention, and a reactivity construct salvaged from Emotional Regulation.

Suggested structural changes:
- Split Emotional Regulation into reactivity (unipolar) + expressiveness (possible bipolar).
- Merge or drop Persuasive Influence into Assertive Communication if redundancy is confirmed.
- Drop Reflective Self-Awareness as a scored axis; reviewer sees it as metacognitive, lacking a legitimate opposite, and vulnerable to self-report circularity.

Potential missing constructs suggested for future architecture review:
- pace/tempo: deliberation ↔ speed of action;
- conflict engagement: approach ↔ avoidance;
- detail ↔ gist processing.

## Response model recommendation

For validated bipolar axes:
- 5-position semantic differential with both anchors visible;
- separate non-scale response `Depends on the situation`;
- separate non-scale response `Haven't encountered this` / cannot assess;
- internal signed coding only as an ordinal convention.

For unipolar constructs:
- ordinary typicality format.

Midpoint should mean narrowly: **both poles are equally true of me**, not context dependence.

## Scoring and analysis

- Ordinal modelling throughout.
- Polychoric EFA initially; graded-response IRT becomes reasonable around N≈300 if the structure survives.
- Ordinal alpha / McDonald's omega rather than naïve Cronbach alpha on short ordinal scales.
- No respondent-facing numbers, 0–100, percentiles, or domain averages.
- Dispersion across three situations is research-only and must not be interpreted as flexibility.

## Validation plan

Reviewer B recommends a more conservative staged plan:
- Stage 0: 3–5 domain experts for pole/content review;
- Stage 1: 12–20 think-aloud cognitive interviews using 8–12 candidate items per construct;
- Stage 2: 60–120 usability/item-distribution pilot;
- Stage 3: 300–500 structural sample as the real go/no-go gate;
- Stage 4: 60–100 test–retest over 3–6 weeks;
- Stage 5: 200–300 external validity sample using an established benchmark such as public-domain Big Five/IPIP;
- Stage 6: independent hold-out/cross-validation for weights/projections;
- Stage 7: separate LT/EN invariance work.

## DISC

**Qualitative lens only initially.**

Reject hand-assigned D/i/S/C mapping. Any future projection requires an empirical two-dimensional solution plus out-of-sample replication. Never imply equivalence with official DiSC.

## PAEI

**Qualitative lens only.**

PAEI should remain an overlapping correspondence lens, not a zero-sum score. Reviewer B is more conservative than Reviewer A and argues that numerical P/A/E/I scores are not defensible in the foreseeable validation path because the target framework itself has limited published psychometric validation.

## Visualization

Reject filled radar polygons. Preferred display:
- horizontal bipolar bars;
- shared neutral line;
- both pole labels visible;
- marker, not fill;
- no respondent-facing numbers.

A circular version is acceptable only as unconnected markers on a neutral ring, not a filled polygon.

## Rejection rules

Restrict or reject bipolarity per axis if:
- opposite ends form separate factors;
- pole items correlate weakly rather than inversely;
- midpoint is dominated by context dependence;
- one pole is systematically more desirable;
- hybrid fit is materially better;
- the structure fails hold-out replication.

Architecture-level fallback: if fewer than about 4 of the candidate bipolar axes survive structural testing, abandon the bipolar architecture as the core design.
