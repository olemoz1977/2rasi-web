# Leadership SJT — Stage 0 Critical Incident protocol

Status: EXPLORATORY RESEARCH PROTOCOL
Date: 2026-08-23
Purpose: collect real first-line managerial incidents before writing the new SJT bank.

This protocol supports the 2rasi research / for-fun phase. It does **not** establish content validity by itself.

---

## 1. Target participant

Preferred participant:
- current or recent front-line manager;
- formal responsibility for other people's work;
- works close to daily operations;
- regularly handles priorities, performance, conflict, delegation, safety, quality or service problems.

Useful secondary participant:
- manager who directly supervises front-line managers.

Avoid as the main Stage 0A sample:
- people with no people responsibility;
- pure project coordinators with no authority;
- senior executives far removed from daily first-line decisions.

---

## 2. Interview length

Target: **15–25 minutes**.

Aim for **2–3 concrete incidents** per participant rather than a long abstract discussion about leadership.

An incident should describe something that actually happened, not what a good manager theoretically should do.

---

## 3. Opening script

Suggested plain-language introduction:

> Kuriame eksperimentinį vadovavimo situacijų įrankį. Man svarbu ne tavo nuomonė apie „gerą lyderystę“, o realios situacijos, kuriose vadovui teko rinktis tarp kelių neidealių sprendimų. Nenaudosime įmonių ar žmonių vardų. Mane domina, kas įvyko, ką vadovas padarė ir kuo baigėsi.

Do not show the proposed competency map before collecting incidents. It would prime the participant to give taxonomy-shaped examples.

---

## 4. Role-context questions

Ask briefly before incidents:

1. Kiek žmonių tiesiogiai ar per kitus vadovauji / vadovavai?
2. Kokio pobūdžio buvo komandos darbas?
3. Kokią realią sprendimo teisę turėjo vadovas?
4. Kokie buvo svarbiausi kasdieniai apribojimai: laikas, žmonės, kokybė, sauga, klientai, įranga, informacija ar kita?
5. Ar vadovas galėjo skirti užduotis, reikalauti atsiskaitymo ir taikyti pasekmes?

These questions are context variables, not scored characteristics.

---

## 5. Incident prompts

Ask for concrete examples. Do not force every participant through every prompt.

### Prompt A — difficult people/performance decision

> Papasakok apie situaciją, kai darbuotojas ar komanda nedavė laukto rezultato ir nebuvo akivaizdu, ką vadovas turėtų daryti.

### Prompt B — competing priorities

> Papasakok apie situaciją, kai vienu metu reikėjo padaryti kelis svarbius dalykus, bet žmonių, laiko ar kitų resursų neužteko viskam.

### Prompt C — conflict / disagreement

> Papasakok apie situaciją, kai žmonės nesutarė, kilo įtampa arba vadovas turėjo pasakyti nepopuliarų dalyką.

### Prompt D — autonomy / delegation

> Papasakok apie situaciją, kai reikėjo nuspręsti, kiek laisvės duoti darbuotojui ar komandai ir kiek pačiam vadovui įsikišti.

### Prompt E — safety / ethics / fairness

> Papasakok apie situaciją, kai greitesnis ar patogesnis sprendimas konfliktavo su sauga, taisyklėmis, sąžiningumu ar vienodu žmonių traktavimu.

### Prompt F — escalation

> Papasakok apie situaciją, kai vadovui reikėjo nuspręsti: spręsti pačiam, įtraukti kitus ar kelti problemą aukščiau.

### Prompt G — change

> Papasakok apie situaciją, kai pasikeitė procesas, taisyklė, technologija ar darbo būdas ir komanda į tai reagavo ne taip, kaip tikėtasi.

These prompts are sampling aids. They are not assumed final competencies.

---

## 6. Follow-up questions for every useful incident

For each incident ask:

1. **Kas konkrečiai atsitiko?**
2. **Kas tuo momentu buvo vadovo atsakomybė?**
3. **Koks buvo svarbiausias apribojimas ar rizika?**
4. **Ko vadovas tuo metu nežinojo arba dėl ko negalėjo būti tikras?**
5. **Kokius realius pasirinkimus jis turėjo?**
6. **Ką jis padarė pirmiausia?**
7. **Kodėl pasirinko būtent tai?**
8. **Kas įvyko po to?**
9. **Kas šiame sprendime buvo gerai?**
10. **Ką, žiūrint atgal, buvo galima padaryti geriau?**
11. **Koks kitas sprendimas būtų buvęs realistiškas, bet prastesnis? Kodėl?**
12. **Ar yra situacija, kur tas „prastesnis“ sprendimas kaip tik būtų buvęs teisingas?**

Question 12 is especially useful: it helps detect whether a future SJT option is genuinely context-dependent or merely an obviously bad distractor.

---

## 7. Incident quality gate

Keep an incident for the scenario pool only if most of these are true:

- it actually happened;
- there was a real managerial decision;
- at least two plausible actions existed;
- the best action depended on context, not on a moral slogan;
- the consequence mattered;
- enough context can be described without naming a company or person;
- the incident can be rewritten so experience in one specific company is not required;
- the incident is not simply a technical-knowledge quiz.

Reject or park incidents where:
- the answer is obvious from law/rule alone and no judgement is involved;
- only one action was realistically possible;
- success depends primarily on specialist technical knowledge;
- the story cannot be anonymised;
- the participant is describing philosophy rather than an event.

---

## 8. Minimal capture template

For every retained incident record:

```text
incident_id:
participant_role_family:
approx_team_size:
formal_people_authority: yes/no
industry_context: optional broad category only
incident_summary:
manager_responsibility:
primary_constraint:
uncertainty:
options_that_were_realistically_available:
action_taken:
rationale_given:
outcome:
what_worked:
what_could_be_better:
credible_alternative_action:
when_that_alternative_might_be_right:
severity: low / medium / high
individual_or_team: individual / team
change_context: steady / change
ethical_safety_fairness_salience: none / present / central
notes:
```

Do not capture:
- names;
- employer-identifying details unless explicit consent and actually necessary;
- confidential process details;
- personal health or other sensitive details unrelated to the managerial judgement.

---

## 9. First-pass coding after collection

Code **after** the interview, never during the participant's story.

Candidate content codes:
- expectations / standards;
- accountability;
- delegation / autonomy;
- coaching / capability development;
- conflict / interpersonal judgement;
- prioritisation;
- escalation;
- ethics / safety / fairness;
- process / operational judgement;
- other / uncategorised.

Context facets:
- individual vs team;
- steady-state vs change;
- time pressure present vs absent;
- high vs low capability of the other party;
- safety/ethical salience;
- high vs low consequence.

`Situational diagnosis` is not coded as a standalone competency. Instead note whether the incident contains meaningful diagnostic ambiguity.

---

## 10. Stage 0A target and output

Exploratory target:
- **6–10 participants**;
- **15–25 usable incidents** minimum;
- preferably at least two organisations / work environments.

This is enough to start seeing repeated scenario families. It is **not** enough to claim a validated job analysis.

Deliverables:
1. anonymised incident library;
2. frequency table of emerging content codes;
3. examples that do not fit the proposed model;
4. missing scenario families;
5. candidate 5-domain blueprint revision;
6. list of incidents that should become SJT stems later.

---

## 11. Stage 0B expansion before serious use

Before the instrument is positioned for selection / promotion:
- expand toward 15–25 interviews;
- include multiple organisations;
- include both incumbents and managers-of-incumbents;
- collect separate importance / frequency / criticality ratings;
- use independent SMEs for construct linkage;
- document disagreement rather than forcing consensus.

Stage 0A is discovery. Stage 0B is the beginning of defensible job-analysis evidence.

---

## 12. Rule before item writing

Do **not** write the final new SJT bank from the proposed competency taxonomy alone.

A future item should ideally be traceable to:

`real incident -> anonymised scenario family -> SME-reviewed options -> pilot item`

The 20 legacy Situational Leadership scenarios may be used as structural inspiration only. Do not reuse them verbatim in any future high-stakes form because they are publicly accessible with a key.
