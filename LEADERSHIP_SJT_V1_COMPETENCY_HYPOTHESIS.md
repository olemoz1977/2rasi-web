# Leadership SJT — V1 competency hypothesis

Status: STAGE 0 / CONSTRUCT HYPOTHESIS / NOT VALIDATED
Date: 2026-08-23

## Purpose

Define a compact set of managerial competencies for a future Leadership SJT before writing or rescoring scenarios.

Working product architecture:

`WorkStyle → Leadership SJT → Role Profile`

PAEI and DISC remain optional later interpretation lenses. They do not define the competency model or scoring.

## Working target role for V1

**Front-line / operational manager** as the first validation target.

Examples:
- team leader;
- shift supervisor;
- production supervisor;
- operational/project lead with direct people responsibility.

This is a working scope, not a permanent product limit.

Why start here:
- the legacy scenario bank already covers many first-line managerial situations;
- the role creates repeated observable decisions about people, priorities and performance;
- it avoids mixing strategic-executive competencies into the first SJT before the core model is validated.

# Proposed competency architecture

## C1. Situational diagnosis

**Question:** Does the manager correctly identify what is actually limiting performance before choosing an intervention?

Signals may include distinguishing:
- lack of skill / knowledge;
- lack of clarity;
- lack of confidence;
- lack of motivation;
- external obstacle;
- competing priority;
- accountability / behaviour problem.

Why it matters:
A technically “good” leadership action can be wrong when applied to the wrong diagnosis.

Important boundary:
This is not the same as WorkStyle `Systems Thinking`. WorkStyle asks how broadly a person tends to look. SJT diagnosis asks whether the person identifies the relevant cause in a concrete managerial situation.

---

## C2. Direction and expectation clarity

**Question:** Can the manager create enough structure for successful execution?

Signals:
- clarifies expected outcome;
- defines standards / boundaries;
- clarifies responsibility;
- sets appropriate follow-up;
- avoids unnecessary micromanagement.

Failure patterns:
- under-directing a novice;
- over-directing a capable employee;
- confusing activity instructions with outcome clarity.

---

## C3. Delegation and autonomy calibration

**Question:** Can the manager give the right amount of ownership for the person's capability and the task risk?

Signals:
- delegates decision rights, not only tasks;
- matches autonomy to capability / risk;
- defines boundaries;
- avoids taking work back unnecessarily;
- avoids premature hands-off delegation.

Important boundary:
WorkStyle `Autonomy ↔ Cooperation` is a preference. This competency is the ability to delegate appropriately even when the manager personally prefers to work independently.

---

## C4. Performance accountability

**Question:** Can the manager address underperformance without avoiding the issue or overreacting?

Signals:
- names the performance gap;
- distinguishes cause from excuse;
- clarifies expectation;
- agrees corrective action;
- follows up proportionately;
- escalates when needed.

Failure patterns:
- excessive accommodation;
- immediate punishment without diagnosis;
- vague encouragement without accountability;
- solving the employee's problem instead of managing performance.

---

## C5. Coaching and development judgement

**Question:** Can the manager help another person build capability rather than only solve the immediate task?

Signals:
- asks diagnostic questions;
- provides usable feedback;
- chooses teaching / coaching / practice appropriately;
- gives increasing autonomy as capability grows;
- makes learning actionable.

Important boundary:
This is managerial capability, not a self-reported `Learning Orientation` preference.

---

## C6. Interpersonal and conflict judgement

**Question:** Can the manager engage disagreement, tension and emotional signals while preserving task accountability?

Signals:
- listens without surrendering responsibility;
- surfaces disagreement rather than avoiding it;
- separates person from issue;
- handles competing interests;
- chooses when to facilitate, decide, or escalate.

Coverage warning:
The legacy 20-item bank has limited direct conflict coverage. New scenarios will be required.

---

## C7. Change and uncertainty leadership

**Question:** Can the manager lead execution when the environment, process, technology or team changes?

Signals:
- assesses capability gaps created by change;
- communicates what is changing and what is not;
- creates implementation structure;
- involves people where useful;
- adjusts support / control as adoption progresses.

Important boundary:
This is not the same as WorkStyle `Adaptability ↔ Consistency`. A manager can naturally prefer stability but still competently lead change.

---

## C8. Priority and decision judgement

**Question:** Can the manager choose what matters first when time, people or information are limited?

Signals:
- identifies critical constraint;
- distinguishes urgent from important;
- decides when a decision is required vs when more diagnosis is worth the delay;
- protects higher-priority work;
- allocates attention / resources proportionately.

Coverage warning:
The legacy bank under-represents genuine priority trade-offs. New scenarios will be required.

---

## C9. Operational / systems judgement — conditional candidate

**Question:** Can the manager see downstream effects and avoid solving one local problem by creating another?

Signals:
- considers process dependencies;
- distinguishes symptom from recurring cause;
- recognises cross-team / cross-process consequences;
- chooses proportionate depth of analysis.

Status:
**Conditional candidate.** It may be essential for operational-manager roles but should not automatically be part of every future leadership role profile.

Important boundary:
This is a competence/judgement construct. It should not be inferred from a WorkStyle self-report preference alone.

---

# Proposed architecture summary

### Core V1 candidates

1. Situational diagnosis
2. Direction and expectation clarity
3. Delegation and autonomy calibration
4. Performance accountability
5. Coaching and development judgement
6. Interpersonal and conflict judgement
7. Change and uncertainty leadership
8. Priority and decision judgement

### Role-conditional candidate

9. Operational / systems judgement

This is intentionally **not** a 15-competency model.

The number of competencies should be determined by content coverage, redundancy review and later data, not inherited symmetry.

# How a future SJT should score evidence

## Do not use one total “leadership percentage” initially

Each scenario can provide evidence for 1–3 competencies.

Example internal coding concept:

| Response | Diagnosis | Accountability | Support | Delegation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A | +2 | +1 | 0 | 0 |
| B | 0 | +2 | -1 | 0 |
| C | +1 | +1 | +2 | +1 |
| D | -1 | 0 | 0 | +2 |

Numbers above are illustrative only.

Actual weights require subject-matter expert review and later empirical calibration.

## Evidence first, classification later

Early output should look like:

- stronger evidence;
- mixed evidence;
- insufficient evidence;
- development risk hypothesis.

Avoid:
- `78% leadership potential`;
- `suitable / unsuitable` from SJT alone;
- hard cut-offs before criterion validation.

---

# Relationship to selection

Leadership SJT should be **one component** of future manager selection, not the whole selection decision.

Potential future battery:

1. **Role context / eligibility** — what leadership work the role actually requires.
2. **WorkStyle** — usual behavioural tendencies.
3. **Leadership SJT** — managerial judgement in realistic situations.
4. **Structured interview / behavioural evidence** — what the candidate has actually done.
5. Optional later work sample / simulation for higher-stakes roles.

This separation allows an important distinction:

> preference is not competence, and competence is not role fit.

# WorkStyle × SJT interaction hypotheses

These combinations are future research hypotheses, not scoring rules.

### Example A

WorkStyle: strong autonomy preference.
SJT: strong delegation judgement.

Interpretation hypothesis:

> Personal preference for independent work does not prevent appropriate delegation as a manager.

### Example B

WorkStyle: strong autonomy preference.
SJT: weak delegation judgement.

Interpretation hypothesis:

> A natural tendency may become a managerial risk when the role requires achieving results through others.

### Example C

WorkStyle: strong accommodating communication preference.
SJT: strong conflict/accountability judgement.

Interpretation hypothesis:

> A harmony preference is not a competence limitation if the person still engages difficult issues appropriately.

No interaction should become a hard selection rule without validation.

# Required Stage 0 review questions

Before writing a new item bank, reviewers / SMEs should answer:

1. Are these 8 core competencies separable in real first-line management work?
2. Which are redundant or missing?
3. Is Situational Diagnosis a separate competency or a meta-process underlying the others?
4. Should conflict judgement be separate from performance accountability?
5. Is Coaching & Development relevant enough for first-line selection, or more appropriate as a development measure?
6. Should Priority / Decision Judgement be split into prioritisation and decision quality?
7. Is Operational / Systems Judgement core for the target role or role-specific?
8. Which competencies are must-have at entry, and which are realistically developable after promotion?
9. What external job-performance criteria could later validate each competency?
