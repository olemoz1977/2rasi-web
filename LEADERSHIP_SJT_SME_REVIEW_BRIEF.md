# Leadership SJT — Practitioner SME Review Brief

Status: STAGE 0 / PRACTITIONER CONTENT REVIEW
Date: 2026-08-23

## Purpose

This review is for experienced managers / supervisors, not psychometric reviewers.

The goal is to test whether the proposed competencies describe **real first-line management work** and whether important managerial situations are missing.

Do not judge statistical validation or psychometric scoring. Judge practical realism, role relevance and behavioural distinctness.

Target role:

**Front-line / operational manager** with direct responsibility for people and day-to-day results.

Examples:
- team leader;
- shift supervisor;
- production supervisor;
- operational/project lead with people responsibility.

---

# Proposed competencies

1. Situational diagnosis
2. Direction and expectation clarity
3. Delegation and autonomy calibration
4. Performance accountability
5. Coaching and development judgement
6. Interpersonal and conflict judgement
7. Change and uncertainty leadership
8. Priority and decision judgement
9. Operational / systems judgement — currently role-conditional

---

# Instructions to reviewer

Please judge the model from actual managerial experience.

For every competency answer:

1. Is this behaviour genuinely important in first-line management?
2. Can you recognise it in observable manager behaviour?
3. Is it meaningfully different from the other competencies?
4. Is it something a newly promoted manager should already demonstrate, or can it reasonably be developed after promotion?
5. Can you remember a real situation where this competency clearly separated a stronger manager from a weaker one?
6. Is the wording understandable without management-theory terminology?

Use one verdict:

- KEEP
- MERGE
- SPLIT
- DROP
- ROLE-SPECIFIC

---

# Critical-incident request

For every competency you KEEP, provide at least one real or realistic critical incident.

Use this structure:

**Situation:** What happened?

**Manager's responsibility:** What did the manager need to achieve?

**Constraint:** What made the decision difficult? Examples: time pressure, weak employee capability, conflict, safety risk, incomplete information, competing priorities.

**Strong response:** What would an effective manager actually do?

**Weak but realistic response:** What might a less effective manager plausibly do?

**Why:** What makes the difference important?

Do not provide obviously foolish distractors. The future SJT needs realistic alternatives that competent people could genuinely disagree about.

---

# Specific questions

## Situational diagnosis

When performance falls, how often does a manager actually need to distinguish between:
- lack of skill;
- lack of clarity;
- lack of motivation;
- lack of confidence;
- external obstacle;
- competing priority;
- accountability problem?

Is this a separate competency, or simply the first step of good management in general?

## Direction and expectation clarity

Is this distinct enough from accountability?

Give examples of:
- under-directing;
- appropriate clarity;
- micromanagement.

## Delegation and autonomy calibration

What information should a manager consider before deciding how much autonomy to give?

Examples: task risk, employee experience, demonstrated capability, urgency, reversibility, safety/compliance.

## Performance accountability

What distinguishes fair accountability from:
- avoidance;
- micromanagement;
- punishment;
- doing the work for the employee?

## Coaching and development

For a newly promoted supervisor, is coaching:
- essential on day one;
- useful but developable;
- secondary to operational control?

Explain why.

## Interpersonal and conflict judgement

What types of conflict should a first-line manager be able to handle?

Examples:
- employee vs employee;
- employee vs manager;
- performance disagreement;
- role/responsibility conflict;
- interpersonal hostility;
- conflict between production/result and people concerns.

## Change and uncertainty leadership

What kinds of change are realistic at first-line level?

Examples: new process, technology, staffing change, target change, schedule change, policy change.

## Priority and decision judgement

Give examples where the manager cannot satisfy every demand and must choose what happens first.

## Operational / systems judgement

Should this be expected from every front-line manager, or only in operational/process-heavy roles?

What distinguishes useful systems awareness from unnecessary over-analysis?

# Missing-content challenge

Which important first-line management behaviours are absent?

Consider, but do not automatically add:
- safety / ethical judgement;
- planning and coordination;
- workload/resource allocation;
- escalation judgement;
- communication;
- conflict engagement;
- decision-making under pressure;
- fairness / consistency;
- employee recognition;
- cross-team coordination;
- customer/stakeholder judgement;
- risk judgement.

List only behaviours that are important enough to affect selection decisions.

# Role-boundary check

For each retained competency indicate whether it should be:

- UNIVERSAL — relevant across most front-line manager roles;
- OPERATIONAL — especially important in production/operations;
- KNOWLEDGE-WORK — especially important in specialist/project environments;
- ROLE-SPECIFIC — should be activated only for some jobs.

This is important because the future product may use a universal core plus role-specific scenario modules.

# Final required output

Please return:

1. Your management background in broad terms only — role level, approximate years, broad sector. Do not include employer-confidential details.
2. Competency table with KEEP / MERGE / SPLIT / DROP / ROLE-SPECIFIC verdicts.
3. Which competencies are must-have before promotion.
4. Which competencies are realistically developable after promotion.
5. Top 5 missing managerial behaviours, if any.
6. At least 8 critical incidents suitable for future scenario development.
7. Any proposed competencies that sound theoretically good but do not match real first-line work.
8. Final verdict: **REALISTIC / NEEDS REVISION / WRONG TARGET MODEL**.

The purpose is not to make the framework look complete. It is to expose what is missing or artificial before scenario writing begins.
