# WorkStyle 15 — Independent Methodological Review

Reviewer stance: adversarial. The proposal is treated as an unvalidated hypothesis.
Date: 2026-08-23

Throughout, claims are tagged:
- **[T]** supported by established measurement theory
- **[I]** reviewer inference / judgement
- **[E]** cannot be decided without empirical data

---

## 1. Executive verdict

**C — PROCEED WITH HYBRID MODEL. Confidence: 68%.**

Full bipolarity (B) is not defensible on current evidence: at least 5 of the 15 constructs have no true opposite, only a "less of it" end, and forcing anchors onto them would manufacture variance rather than measure it. Retaining pure unipolarity (A) is also not defensible: the cognitive pilots already documented that several reverse items measure *a different legitimate preference* rather than the absence of the target — which is the classic empirical signature of a construct that is genuinely bipolar and was being mis-modelled. **[I]**

However — and this is the more consequential recommendation — the hybrid verdict is conditional on a **substantial partial redesign (elements of D)**. The number 15 should not survive review intact. See §6.

**Main source of uncertainty:** there is currently *no quantitative data at all* on this instrument — only cognitive-pilot friction evidence. Bipolarity is an empirical property of a covariance structure, not of language. Every construct-level verdict below is a prior, not a finding. If Stage 3 data show that the two ends of the "strong bipolar" candidates load as separate factors, the hybrid shrinks toward A. **[E]**

## 2. Fifteen-axis bipolarity audit

Verdict key: **SB** strong bipolar · **PB** possible bipolar · **U** should remain unipolar · **S/R** split or replace

| # | Construct | Proposed pole pair | Verdict | Rationale | Alternative |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Goal Persistence | Persistence ↔ Reprioritisation | **PB** | Genuine trade-off under fixed time, but rational reprioritisation is a *competence*, not the low end. Risk: one pole reads as flakiness. **[I]** | Reframe as **Commitment stability ↔ Responsiveness to new information**. Keep only if anchors are effort-allocation, not virtue. |
| 2 | Work Structure | Structure ↔ Improvisation | **SB** | Well-precedented continuum (order/flexibility). Shared behavioural space: how a task is approached before starting. **[T]** | — |
| 3 | Achievement Drive | Drive ↔ Sufficiency | **U** | "Enough is enough" is not the opposite of striving; it is a threshold rule. A person can be high-drive *and* satisficing on low-value tasks. | 4 | Systems Thinking | Systemic ↔ Local focus | **PB** | Legitimate levels of analysis, but at the *ability* level they correlate positively, not inversely. Only bipolar if framed as attentional default under time pressure. **[I]** | **Default level of analysis when a problem appears** — explicitly a preference, not a capability. |
| 5 | Adaptability | Adaptability ↔ Consistency | **SB** | Strongest candidate. Both poles have obvious functional value; the trade-off is real and situational. **[I]** | — |
| 6 | Learning Orientation | Learning ↔ Proven methods | **PB** | Partly independent: experts often score high on both. Risk of a hidden desirability gradient toward "learning". **[I]** | Reframe as **novel-method exploration ↔ proven-method exploitation** — the explore/exploit trade-off is a defensible single continuum. |
| 7 | Cooperation | Cooperation ↔ Autonomy | **SB** | Supported by the pilots' own evidence. Behavioural space (how a decision gets made) is shared. **[I]** | — |
| 8 | Empathic Attention | Empathy ↔ Factual cues | **U** | Both can be simultaneously high; this is attentional *breadth*, not direction. Also carries the strongest desirability asymmetry in the set. **[I]** | Retain unipolar, or move to a separate **cue-attention** construct measuring what is noticed, not which is preferred. |
| 9 | Intellectual Humility | Humility ↔ Conviction | **PB** | A real continuum only if the poles are *openness to revision* vs *stability of position*. Currently at risk of contrasting a virtue with a vice. **[I]** | **Position revision threshold** — how much counter-evidence changes a stance. |
| 10 | Assertive Communication | Assertive ↔ Accommodating | **SB** | Classic Agency-pole trade-off with circumplex precedent. **[T]** | — |

| 12 | Social Energy | Social ↔ Reserved | **SB** | Best-established bipolar construct in the entire set; extraversion is empirically bipolar across decades of work. **[T]** | — |
| 13 | Emotional Regulation | Regulation ↔ Expression | **U** | Two orthogonal constructs forced into one scale. Suppression ≠ regulation; well-regulated people can be highly expressive. | 14 | Constructive Outlook | Optimism ↔ Risk focus | **PB** | Promising framing, but optimism and risk-detection are empirically separable (defensive pessimism). | 15 | Reflective Self-Awareness | Self-reflection ↔ External focus | **U** | Attention direction is not zero-sum in the way the axis implies; high self-awareness predicts *better* external reading. Bipolarising this manufactures an artefact. **[I]** | Retain unipolar or drop entirely (see §6). |

**Tally:** 5 SB · 5 PB · 5 U. A fully bipolar architecture is unsupported. **[I]**

## 3. Top five methodological strengths

1. **The document argues against itself.** Explicitly refusing to retrospectively rescore v0.5/v0.6 data as bipolar (§11) is the single most credible thing in the proposal. That transformation is invalid — a "1" on a unipolar item does not endorse a specific opposite — and most projects do it anyway. **[T]**
2. **"Cannot assess" separated from the midpoint.** Correct and non-obvious. Collapsing no-experience into the neutral point injects systematic bias toward zero and inflates apparent balance. **[T]**
3. **Refusal to average signed axes into domain scores** without a demonstrated higher-order factor. This is where most commercial instruments quietly fail. **[T]**
4. **Direction/dispersion distinction, correctly hedged.** Recognising that `-2,0,+2` ≠ `0,0,0` *and* that dispersion over three items is unreliable is a sophisticated pairing; the caveat matters more than the insight. **[T]**
5. **Deferring visualization until after the measurement model.** Visual-first design is the standard failure mode for tools of this kind, and the proposal explicitly names the "outer ring implies scientific integration" risk. **[I]**

---

## 4. Top five methodological risks

1. **Language-level bipolarity mistaken for structural bipolarity.** The audit table is built from semantic plausibility. Antonymic labels routinely fail to produce inverse item covariance. This is the central threat to the whole architecture. **[T]**
2. **Desirability asymmetry is not solved by declaring neutrality.** Stating that "neither pole is better" does not neutralise it for the respondent. At least #6, #8, #9, #14 have an obvious "good" end in an organisational context; a work-context instrument amplifies this because respondents imagine an employer reading it. **[T]**
3. **Semi-ipsative distortion.** The format is not formally forced-choice, but a two-anchor scale imposes a trade-off the respondent may not experience. Someone genuinely high on both poles has no honest response except the midpoint — where they are indistinguishable from someone low on both. **This is the strongest single argument against Model B.** **[T]**
4. **Three items per axis will not sustain the claims.** Three ordinal items yield reliability typically too low for individual-level directional feedback, and cannot identify a factor without cross-axis constraints. Fine for a reflection tool; not fine for anything worded as a result. **[T]**
5. **15 axes is an inherited number, not a derived one.** Nothing in either document justifies 15 on structural grounds. §3 of the hypothesis defends 15 against a numerical objection nobody raised, which functions as a distraction from the real question: does the data support 15 separable dimensions? Almost certainly not — see §6. **[I]**

Additional: **midpoint over-determination.** Four distinct meanings currently map to one response — balanced, indifferent, context-dependent, uncertain. Uncertainty is handled; the other three are not. **[T]**

## 5. Answers to the specific questions posed

**Bipolar methodology appropriate?** Partially. Semantic-differential format is appropriate *where bipolarity is real* (#2, #5, #7, #10, #12). Applying it elsewhere converts a measurement question into a forced trade-off and produces uninterpretable data. **[T]**

**Three items per axis?** Adequate for a public reflective tool *only* if reduced from a validated bank of 8–12 candidates, and only if respondent-facing output is qualitative (directional language, not scores). Not adequate for structural claims, test–retest claims, or any PAEI/DISC projection. **[T]**

**5 vs 7 positions?** Test 5 first. With semantic-differential anchors, 5 positions are cognitively cleaner in a second language and reduce spurious precision; 7 gains little when reliability is already limited by 3 items. If Stage 2 shows end-pole piling, revisit. **[I]**

**Signed −2…+2 coding?** Defensible as an internal convention, not as an interval claim. Model responses as ordinal (polychoric / graded-response) from the start; the sign is a display and interpretation convenience only. **[T]**

**Midpoint meaning — recommendation:** define it narrowly as *"both are equally true of me"* and give **context-dependence its own explicit response option**, distinct from both the midpoint and from "cannot assess". Reason: context-dependence is likely to be the single most common honest answer for a work-style instrument, and burying it in the midpoint destroys the interpretability of the entire neutral zone. This is a stronger position than the hypothesis document takes. **[I]**

**Direction vs context-variability modelled separately?** Yes — but as a **research diagnostic only**, never respondent-facing at three items per axis. Dispersion across three scenarios is confounded with unreliability and scenario-content differences; presenting it as "flexibility" would be an unearned interpretation. Revisit only if a scenario-facet design is built in deliberately (same axis × systematically varied context), which would require 6–9 items per axis. **[T]**

**Risks of artificial poles:** the specific damage is that a manufactured opposite pole attracts *its own* legitimate variance (autonomy, stability, risk analysis — exactly what the pilots observed), so the axis measures a blend of two constructs and its "score" has no single referent. Items then show poor local fit and the axis appears unreliable when in fact it is misspecified. **[T]**

**Domains:** demote all five to descriptive groupings with **no numerical score** until a higher-order structure is empirically demonstrated. Averaging signed axes with different semantic directions is arithmetically possible and psychologically meaningless. **[T]**

## 6. Recommended architecture

**Hybrid, reduced — target 9–11 constructs, not 15.**

- **Bipolar core (5):** Work Structure, Adaptability↔Consistency, Cooperation↔Autonomy, Assertive↔Accommodating, Social Energy. These get semantic-differential items.
- **Conditional bipolar (4), pending Stage 1 pole review:** Goal Persistence, Explore↔Exploit (redesigned Learning Orientation), Position Revision (redesigned Intellectual Humility), Opportunity↔Risk scanning (redesigned Constructive Outlook).
- **Unipolar (2–3):** Achievement Drive, Empathic Attention, plus a reactivity scale salvaged from Emotional Regulation.
- **Split:** Emotional Regulation → reactivity (unipolar) + expressiveness (bipolar candidate).
- **Merge or drop:** Persuasive Influence into Assertive Communication (likely redundant — both are Agency-pole influence behaviours). **[I]**
- **Drop:** Reflective Self-Awareness as a scored axis. It is metacognitive rather than stylistic, it has no legitimate opposite, and it is the construct most contaminated by self-report circularity (using self-report to measure accuracy of self-report). **[I]**

**Redundancy flags for empirical testing:** #10/#11 (near-certain overlap), #4/#6 (both tap analytical exploration), #1/#5 (persistence vs adaptability may be one axis with two names). **[E]**

**Missing constructs [I]:** the set has no coverage of (a) **pace/tempo** — deliberation vs speed of action, which is orthogonal to structure and heavily loaded in most work-style models; (b) **conflict engagement** — approach vs avoidance, distinct from assertiveness; (c) **detail vs gist processing**. If a construct-architecture redesign happens, these are stronger candidates than #15.

## 7. Recommended response and scoring model

- **Format:** 5-position semantic differential with both anchors visible, plus two non-scale options: *"Depends on the situation"* and *"Haven't encountered this."*
- **Scoring:** ordinal throughout. Polychoric correlations; graded response model (IRT) is *not* premature if N reaches ~300 — it is the correct model for signed ordinal data and yields item information curves that directly test whether both poles discriminate. Below that N, stick to polychoric EFA. **[T]**
- **Reliability:** ordinal alpha / McDonald's ω, not Cronbach's alpha on 3 ordinal items. Report with confidence intervals; expect them to be wide and say so. **[T]**
- **Missing data:** listwise at axis level; report per-item missing rates as a primary Stage 2 outcome. Do not impute to midpoint. **[T]**
- **Respondent output:** directional language and a neutral zone. **No numbers, no 0–100, no percentiles.** **[I]**

## 8. Validation plan

| Stage | Sample | Justified claims | Not justified |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 — Pole review | 3–5 domain experts | Face/content validity of anchors | Anything empirical |
| 1 — Cognitive interviews | 12–20, think-aloud, 8–12 items/axis | Item comprehension, ambiguity, desirability perception | Structure, reliability |
| 2 — Usability pilot | 60–120 | Completion, missing rates, endorsement distributions, item redundancy screening | Factor structure |
| 3 — Structural | **300–500 minimum** for 9–11 axes | EFA/parallel analysis, ESEM, local dependence, whether poles are inverse or separate | Norms, individual feedback |
| 4 — Test–retest | 60–100, 3–6 week interval | Stability; distinguishes trait from state | — |
| 5 — External validity | 200–300 with an established measure (a public-domain Big Five/IPIP set is the practical benchmark) | Convergent/discriminant validity | DISC/PAEI equivalence |
| 6 — Cross-validation | Independent holdout | Any weights, cut-points, projections | Generalisation beyond sampled population |
| 7 — Invariance | Separate LT/EN samples | Language comparability | — |

Realistic timeline for a small independent project: Stages 0–2 are achievable; Stage 3 at N=300+ is the genuine bottleneck and should be treated as the go/no-go gate for every downstream claim. **[I]**

**DISC verdict:** *feasible only as a qualitative lens, and only post-Stage 5.* A hand-assigned D/i/S/C block mapping should be rejected outright. An empirical projection would require the benchmark data, a two-dimensional solution that actually emerges, and out-of-sample replication. Until then, label it exploratory and never imply equivalence with any commercial instrument. **[T][I]**

**PAEI verdict:** *qualitative lens only.* The four-overlapping-projections framing is correct and the refusal to force a sum to 100% is correct. But PAEI has thin published psychometric validation of its own, so a WorkStyle→PAEI mapping cannot inherit credibility from the target. Numerical P/A/E/I scores are not defensible at any foreseeable stage of this project. **[I]**

**Visualization:** reject the conventional radar polygon — filled area implies magnitude and "bigger = better", which is exactly wrong for signed axes. Recommend **horizontal bipolar bars, grouped by domain, with a shared vertical neutral line**, both pole labels always visible, and a marker rather than a fill. This handles 9–15 axes without clutter, gives both poles equal visual weight, requires no numbers, and reads correctly on a phone. If a circular form is required for brand reasons, use spoke markers on a neutral ring with no connecting polygon — but the linear form is perceptually superior. **[I]**

## 9. Conditions for rejecting the bipolar hypothesis

Reject or restrict per-axis if, at Stage 3:

1. Items from the two ends load on **separate factors** rather than one bidirectional factor → that axis is two constructs. **Decisive.**
2. Pole-pair items correlate **weakly (|r| < ~.30)** rather than inversely → no shared continuum.
3. **Midpoint exceeds ~35–40%** of responses *and* Stage 1 interviews show it is chosen for context-dependence rather than balance → the neutral zone is uninterpretable.
4. A **desirability check** (social-desirability scale or expert "which pole looks better at work" rating) shows systematic one-sided drift → the axis measures impression management.
5. A **hybrid model fits substantially better** on ordinal CFA/ESEM comparison → adopt the hybrid per-axis.
6. The axis structure **fails to replicate** in the holdout sample.
7. Any DISC/PAEI projection is **unstable out of sample** → drop the layer, do not retune it.

Reject the *architecture as a whole* (fall back to A, or move to D) if fewer than 4 of the 9 candidate bipolar axes survive criteria 1–2. **[I]**

## 10. Decision before the next item bank

**REVISE — do not write v0.7 items yet.**

Writing items now would encode 15 inherited constructs into a new data series before Stage 0 has determined which of them exist. Complete the pole review and the redundancy merges first; the cost of writing 45 items against a structure that loses a third of its constructs is a wasted pilot.

## Final verdict

**PROCEED WITH HYBRID MODEL — confidence 68%.**

The 32% is not hedging about hybrid-versus-bipolar. It is the absence of any covariance data. Bipolarity is a property of how items behave together, and no items in this architecture have ever behaved together. Every verdict above — including the five "strong bipolar" designations — is a theory-driven prior that Stage 3 is entitled to overturn. The proposal's own §11 makes exactly this point about its predecessor data; the same discipline applies to this review.
