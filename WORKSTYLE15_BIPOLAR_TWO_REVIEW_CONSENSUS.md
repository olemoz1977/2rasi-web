# WorkStyle 15 — two-review methodology consensus

Status: ARCHITECTURE REVIEW / PRE-STAGE-0
Date: 2026-08-23

This document synthesizes two independent reviews of the proposed WorkStyle 15 bipolar architecture.

Reviewer A verdict: **PROCEED WITH HYBRID MODEL**, confidence 72%.
Reviewer B verdict: **PROCEED WITH HYBRID MODEL**, confidence 68%.

The reviews were conducted independently from the same hypothesis and review brief. The purpose of this synthesis is to separate robust agreement from unresolved design questions.

---

## 1. High-confidence consensus

Both reviewers independently agree on the following:

1. **Do not convert all 15 constructs into bipolar axes.**
2. **Do not retain pure unipolarity either.** Several reverse items in the existing pilots appear to capture legitimate alternative work styles rather than merely the absence of a target trait.
3. **Use a hybrid architecture.** Bipolar format only where both poles are legitimate alternatives within the same behavioural space.
4. **The number 15 is not sacred.** It is inherited from the current architecture and should not constrain redesign if redundancy or missing constructs are identified.
5. **Do not retrospectively rescore v0.5/v0.6 as bipolar.** Those data remain cognitive-pilot evidence only.
6. **Do not show 0–100 scores, percentiles or normative-looking numbers to respondents in the new architecture.**
7. **Do not average signed axes into domain scores** until a higher-order structure is empirically demonstrated.
8. **Separate midpoint from context-dependence and from cannot-assess/no-experience.**
9. **Use 5 response positions first** for bipolar semantic-differential items.
10. **Three final items per construct may be acceptable for a reflective product only after reduction from a larger candidate bank.** Initial item development should start substantially wider.
11. **DISC and PAEI must remain correspondence lenses, not architecture drivers.** Hand-assigned numerical mappings are not justified.
12. **Reject the conventional filled radar polygon** for signed bipolar results because it visually implies outward = more/better.
13. **Do not write the next production item bank before construct/pole review.** Stage 0 comes first.

## 2. Strongest bipolar consensus

Five constructs received strong-bipolar support from both reviewers:

### Work Structure
Candidate framing: **Structure ↔ Improvisation / emergent organisation**

Why it survives conceptually:
- both poles describe legitimate ways of approaching work;
- they occupy the same behavioural space;
- neither pole must be framed as failure or competence deficit.

### Adaptability
Candidate framing: **Adaptability ↔ Consistency / stability**

Why it survives conceptually:
- both poles can be functional depending on context;
- the trade-off is interpretable as preference/default, not competence.

### Cooperation
Candidate framing: **Cooperation ↔ Autonomy**

Why it survives conceptually:
- existing cognitive pilots already exposed that autonomy is not simply low cooperation;
- both poles describe how work/decisions are approached.

### Assertive Communication
Candidate framing: **Assertive ↔ Accommodating / harmony-preserving**

Why it survives conceptually:
- both are legitimate interpersonal strategies;
- clear circumplex/agency precedent exists conceptually.

### Social Energy
Candidate framing: **Socially active ↔ Reserved participation**

Why it survives conceptually:
- clear behavioural continuum;
- neither pole needs to be morally loaded.

These five form the current **highest-confidence bipolar core**, but bipolarity remains an empirical hypothesis until covariance data exist.

---

## 3. Consensus unipolar / do-not-force-bipolar constructs

Four constructs were judged unsuitable for forced bipolarisation by both reviews:

### Achievement Drive
Problem: **Drive ↔ Sufficiency** confounds striving with rational threshold-setting/efficiency.

Current direction: keep unipolar or split into clearer subconstructs such as standard height and effort intensity.

### Empathic Attention
Problem: empathy and factual/explicit-cue focus can both be high simultaneously.

Current direction: keep unipolar or redesign as cue-attention breadth rather than a forced preference axis.

### Emotional Regulation
Problem: regulation and emotional expression are not opposites. A highly regulated person can also be expressive.

Current direction: split candidate constructs, e.g. reactivity and expressiveness, rather than forcing one bipolar axis.

### Reflective Self-Awareness
Problem: reflection and external/action focus can coexist; self-reporting self-awareness also creates circularity concerns.

Current direction: retain as unipolar only if clearly justified, use as a non-scored reflective/meta layer, or drop as a scored WorkStyle construct.

## 4. Conditional / disputed constructs

These require Stage 0 conceptual work before item writing.

### Goal Persistence
Both reviews: possible bipolar, but current opposite is weak.

Better candidate question:
**Commitment stability ↔ responsiveness to new information / strategic switching**

Core risk: rational reprioritisation must not be coded as low persistence or flakiness.

### Systems Thinking
Reviewer A: strong bipolar.
Reviewer B: possible bipolar only if framed as an attentional default rather than ability.

Candidate framing:
**System-level default ↔ local/concrete default**

Open question: can a person be high at both levels of analysis? If yes, this is not a true bipolar trait unless context is constrained.

### Learning Orientation
Both reviews: possible bipolar, but `learning ↔ proven methods` risks desirability asymmetry and independence.

Better candidate framing:
**Exploration of new methods ↔ exploitation of proven methods**

This may be a more defensible trade-off than the current construct label.

### Intellectual Humility
Both reviews: possible bipolar only if reframed.

Better candidate framing:
**Readiness to revise position ↔ stability of position / high revision threshold**

Risk: never frame conviction as dogmatism or humility as virtue.

### Constructive Outlook
Reviewer A: strong bipolar.
Reviewer B: possible bipolar.

Better candidate framing:
**Opportunity scanning ↔ risk scanning as first attentional response**

Risk: optimism and risk detection may be independent; defensive pessimism is a plausible counterexample.

### Persuasive Influence
Reviewer A: possible bipolar.
Reviewer B: unipolar and possible merge with Assertive Communication.

Open question: does this construct carry unique variance beyond assertiveness/agency, or should it be absorbed/renamed?

---

## 5. Architecture implication

The two reviews do **not** justify an immediate reduction to a fixed number, but they do justify abandoning the assumption that all current 15 dimensions must survive.

Current safest architecture stance:

- **5 high-confidence bipolar candidates**;
- **4 high-confidence non-bipolar constructs**;
- **6 unresolved constructs** requiring conceptual redesign, merge, split or empirical decision.

Therefore the next architecture may plausibly end in approximately 9–13 scored constructs, but no exact target should be fixed before Stage 0.

Reviewer B explicitly recommends a 9–11 construct target. Reviewer A is less aggressive about reduction. This remains an unresolved design choice, not a consensus finding.

---

## 6. Missing-construct challenge raised by Review B

Review B identifies three potentially missing work-style areas:

1. **Pace / tempo** — deliberation ↔ speed of action;
2. **Conflict engagement** — approach ↔ avoidance, potentially distinct from assertiveness;
3. **Detail ↔ gist processing**.

These are hypotheses for architecture review, not automatic additions. Stage 0 should compare their conceptual value against weaker inherited constructs such as Reflective Self-Awareness and possibly Persuasive Influence.

---

## 7. Response-model consensus

### Bipolar items
Recommended format:
- five-position semantic differential;
- both behavioural anchors visible;
- internal signed coding may use `-2, -1, 0, +1, +2`;
- signed coding is a convention, not proof of interval measurement.

Midpoint should mean something narrow such as:
**both descriptions fit me about equally**.

Separate non-scale options:
- **Depends on the situation**;
- **Have not encountered / cannot assess this**.

### Unipolar items
Use a simple workplace-typicality format where needed.

Avoid using a reverse item merely to satisfy a mechanical keying ratio. A reverse statement is only useful if it still measures the same construct and does not introduce a new legitimate preference.

---

## 8. Scoring / analysis consensus

For early research:
- treat responses as ordinal;
- use polychoric correlations / ordinal EFA where sample size permits;
- use McDonald's omega / ordinal reliability estimates rather than relying on raw Cronbach alpha;
- track missing/cannot-assess/context-response rates as item-quality signals;
- keep context variability as research metadata, not a respondent-facing trait with only three final items.

Do not create numerical domain scores unless data support a higher-order factor.

Do not create numerical DISC or PAEI scores from theory-only weights.

---

## 9. PAEI and DISC consensus

### DISC
Both reviews agree:
- no manual mapping of WorkStyle constructs into D/i/S/C blocks;
- possible future empirical correspondence only;
- any projection requires an established benchmark and out-of-sample stability;
- until then, use qualitative language only and do not imply equivalence with commercial DiSC.

### PAEI
Both reviews agree:
- P/A/E/I should not be forced to sum to 100%;
- possible qualitative overlapping lens;
- numerical PAEI projection is not justified without empirical evidence;
- PAEI should never determine the number or arrangement of WorkStyle constructs.

---

## 10. Visualization consensus

Reject:
- conventional filled radar polygon;
- any display where outward automatically looks stronger/better;
- hidden or unlabeled opposite poles.

Preferred current research direction:
- horizontal bipolar bars with a common neutral line;
- both pole labels visible;
- marker, not filled magnitude;
- group constructs visually by descriptive domain if useful;
- circular brand-oriented visualization may be explored later using neutral-ring/spoke markers without a connecting polygon.

No final visualization decision before the measurement architecture stabilizes.

---

## 11. Validation staging consensus

### Stage 0 — construct and pole review
Do this now.

Goals:
- define each candidate construct behaviourally;
- test whether both proposed poles belong to the same behavioural space;
- identify desirability asymmetry;
- merge, split, retain or drop constructs before writing a large bank.

### Stage 1 — cognitive item development
Create a larger candidate bank, approximately 8–12 items per surviving construct, then use cognitive interviews to reduce ambiguity and social desirability.

### Stage 2 — usability / distribution pilot
Collect enough responses to inspect completion burden, missing/context rates, endorsement distributions and obvious redundancy.

### Stage 3 — structural go/no-go
A materially larger sample is required before claims about factor structure or bipolarity.

Reviewer A suggested approximately 150–250 for an initial structural pilot.
Reviewer B recommends 300–500 for the reduced 9–11 construct model.

Consensus: the project should plan for **at least ~300** if it wants structural claims strong enough to guide architecture, with larger samples preferable depending on item count/model complexity.

Only after this stage should downstream scoring and correspondence layers be considered.

---

## 12. Immediate product decision

**Do not write v0.7 as another 45-item questionnaire yet.**

Freeze v0.6 as cognitive-pilot history.

The next WorkStyle artifact should be a **Stage 0 construct architecture**, not a respondent test.

For each current construct create:
1. neutral construct/axis name;
2. behavioural definition;
3. candidate left pole, if any;
4. candidate right pole, if any;
5. evidence that both can be functional;
6. reason they may or may not be true opposites;
7. desirability risks;
8. overlap with other constructs;
9. relation to possible DISC/PAEI lenses, labelled theoretical only;
10. decision: bipolar candidate / unipolar / split / merge / drop / unresolved.

Only after that review should a new item-development version be named.

---

## Consensus verdict

**PROCEED WITH A HYBRID ARCHITECTURE REVIEW, NOT WITH A FULL BIPOLAR TEST.**

The two independent reviews agree strongly enough to stop treating `15 unipolar dimensions × 3 Likert items` as the assumed endpoint, but they do not justify prematurely replacing it with `15 bipolar axes`.

The next uncertainty to resolve is construct architecture, not wording.
